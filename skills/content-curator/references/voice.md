# Voice Mechanics — Writing as Danoosh

The register: **warm, conversational, human-centered.** The energy is trusted peer advisor, coach, and teacher braided together: a strategist a few steps ahead who is visibly still experimenting and genuinely rooting for the reader. He talks WITH people, never down to them, never at them. Coaching energy means questions that open thinking, not answers that close it. Teaching energy means making the complex feel simple without ever making the reader feel small. The reader is a Jim, Stefan, Todd, or Cindy: credentialed, allergic to hype, hungry for the next tier. Respect their intelligence in every sentence, and let them feel seen before they feel taught.

Emotional intent: recognition ("that's exactly my week"), then reframe ("I've been looking at this wrong"), then quiet activation. Never "you're behind," never fear-first, never lecture.

A quick self-check while drafting: read the sentence aloud. If it sounds like advice delivered from a stage, rewrite it as something said across a table. If it explains something the reader obviously knows, cut it. If it tells them what to think instead of showing what you saw, show instead.

## Low cognitive load (the executive reading condition)

The reader is between meetings, on a phone, with ten things pulling at them. Sharpness and simplicity are the same job, not a tradeoff: the insight should be original enough to stop them and plain enough to absorb in one pass. One idea per piece. No nested logic, no stacked abstractions, no setup that takes three paragraphs to pay off. If the exec can't retell the core line to a colleague an hour later, simplify until they can.

Two more reader facts that shape the writing: executives are women and men, so composites and examples reflect that (never male-default). And Danoosh's clients read his feed, so every client-derived piece is written for the source's eyes too: composite for rooms, tribute register for individuals, the reader as protagonist, never the client as cautionary tale.

## Rhythm and formatting

- Paragraphs of 1–3 sentences; single-sentence paragraphs are the default rhythm.
- Sentences average 8–18 words; mix in short punches (3–7 words). Nothing over 30.
- 2–4 standalone fragments per piece for emphasis ("That changed everything.").
- **No em dashes.** Commas or periods. This is a hard brand rule and an AI tell.
- Parentheticals for warmth and asides, 2–4 per piece, ≤12 words each.
- Bold sparingly, key phrases only. Arrow bullets (→) for takeaways; one bullet should break the pattern with voice.
- 1–3 genuine questions per piece; end with one that centers the reader.

## Moves that work (use, don't stack)

- **Anchor with a timestamp.** "Tuesday afternoon." "Fifteen minutes into the call." Signals: this really happened.
- **Ground the abstract.** Every claim gets a photographable detail: the shop floor, the quoting spreadsheet, the kitchen counter, the browser tab.
- **Confession at the peer level.** Admit the struggle a leader would recognize ("AI buried me for three weeks"), not novice anxiety. Trust is earned by honesty about hard things, not smallness.
- **"Here's the real [X]" pivot.** Once per piece, max, to deliver the core reframe.
- **Direct address pivot.** Shift from "I" to "you" only after the story has earned it, usually the final third.
- **The live wire.** One charged detail per piece: a stat that stings ("confidence rose as accuracy fell"), a client's verbatim sentence, a phrase with teeth. Restraint around it. Hold the heat; do not spray it everywhere.
- **Name the sophisticated reference once, lightly.** Jevons paradox, a Wharton study, an MIT stat. Brainy sparkle, one touch, never a lecture. Always paired with the honest step-down ("that research is about enterprises; your shop isn't that, but the gap is the same").

## Structure blueprints

**Personal insight arc:** hook (specific moment or counterintuitive claim) → stakes → turn ("Then I tried something different") → what actually happened, concretely → "here's the real shift" → direct-address close with a question or one practical move.

**Concept reframe:** conventional wisdom → the friction everyone feels → the reframe, in a fragment → evidence from a real client or Danoosh's own work → what changes if you adopt it → open loop.

**LinkedIn post:** hook in the first two lines (before the fold, roughly the first 210 characters), short paragraphs, one idea, core insight ~60% through, soft close. Don't open the hook with "I" as the first word; lead with the moment, the claim, or the other person. Soft CTA at most; the Diagnostic is the only door, and most posts don't need a door at all. When the whole idea is one killer quote, the quote is the hook and the body stays short but developed, giving the reader something to sit with rather than scroll past. Avoid the generic-AI shape (clean framework, tidy bullets, four steps, CTA): every piece carries at least one thing only Danoosh could have written, a verbatim line, a real moment, a specific number.

## The endings rule

The hook writes a check the ending must cash. If the hook promises a turn ("at first"), the climb-out must be shown concretely, one real thing someone built or did, not described abstractly. Land once; do not restate the close three ways.

## Slop patterns (kill on sight)

The banned list catches words; this catches shapes. AI slop is technically competent, structurally correct, and completely forgettable, and readers like Jim now recognize it in half a scroll. Any of these shapes fails the Taste Gate regardless of how clean the sentences are.

- **The generic opening.** Any first line that could be pasted onto a different topic and still work ("Here's the thing about AI...", "If you're like most leaders..."). Danoosh opens with the moment, the claim, or the other person.
- **Hedge-word soup.** "It's important to consider," "there are many factors at play," "it remains to be seen." If deleting the sentence loses nothing, it was soup. He has a position or he doesn't publish.
- **The listicle blob.** Vague inspirational opener, five to seven common-sense "insights," "What do you think? 👇" close. No names, no numbers, no moment. This is the AI Productivity Gospel's native format; nothing from this house may resemble it, even accidentally.
- **False contrarianism.** "Unpopular opinion:" followed by something everyone agrees with, or the consensus stated as rebellion. Real conviction is a position a smart peer could argue with; performed boldness is a tell. Confession at the peer level is the honest alternative.
- **Summary voice.** Reads like a summary of other content rather than original thought: evenly weighted paragraphs that cover every angle and commit to none, no first-person observation, Wikipedia with a casual tone. The fix is witness, not wordsmithing; go back to what Danoosh actually saw.
- **Engagement farming.** Questions he doesn't care about the answer to, manufactured controversy with no real position, "Agree or disagree?" before staking anything. Proximity readers can smell it, and they are the only readers who count.

## Banned outright

"Leverage" (verb), "unlock," "game-changer," "transform" without specifics, "delve," "in today's fast-paced world," "at the end of the day," "it's important to note," "revolutionary," "synergy," "robust," "learnings," "at scale," false urgency, manufactured scarcity, "not X, but Y" contrast-gimmick stacking, performative vulnerability, exclamation points beyond one, em dashes anywhere.

## Preflight (fast)

- Hook creates curiosity in two lines?
- One idea, one live wire?
- Abstract claims grounded in photographable specifics?
- Peer register throughout (would Jim feel respected)?
- Fragments and parentheticals present; paragraphs short?
- Ends once, with the reader as protagonist?
- Banned-phrase scan clean, zero em dashes, no slop shapes?
- Screenshot test: one line a reader would screenshot and send to a peer?
- Onlyness: at least one thing only Danoosh could have written (verbatim line, real moment, specific number)?
- Client story anonymized + aspiration-vs-practice verified?
