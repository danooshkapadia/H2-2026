# 5. Decision-making frameworks and patterns

## 5.1 Freedom-Alignment Test
A major decision filter includes:
- does this maintain or expand freedom?
- does this align with highest vision and strengths?
- does this allow him to be fully seen?
- does this create long-term leverage rather than short-term obligation?

Then comes an expansion layer:
- sovereignty
- visibility
- compounding effect
- authentic energy

Tie-breaker:
- which path gets him closer to his ultimate vision with less regret?

Final heuristic:
- if it is not a full-body yes, it is a no

## 5.2 Leverage Path Test
He also has a more unified decision framework oriented toward:
- reality check
- path test
- replacement question
- capture rule

This points to a consistent bias toward:
- leverage
- clarity
- replacement of reactive urgency with intentional choice

## 5.3 Embodied filtering
He does not decide purely by spreadsheets or formal analysis.
A repeated pattern is checking:
- does this feel expansive?
- does this feel constricting?
- does this preserve sovereignty?
- will I regret saying yes to this?

## 5.4 Aversion to false yeses
He seems increasingly aware that many superficially attractive opportunities are actually traps if they:
- reduce freedom
- hide his value
- create linear effort without equity or leverage
- force him into a role that is too small for his strengths

## 5.5 Likely failure modes in decision-making
When things go sideways, likely causes include:
- urgency overriding alignment
- idea-excitement overriding capacity reality
- taking work that pays now but does not compound
- saying yes because of possibility rather than fit
- being seduced by novelty
- avoiding clear no’s because of people-pleasing or ambivalence
