# Session-Specific Notes

*Updated after each cohort. These notes capture institutional knowledge about what works, what doesn't, and what to watch for in each session. Read this file during Step 4 (keep/cut/compress decisions) and Step 7 (session architecture design).*

## Session 1: Orientation & Mindsets

- Students arrive intimidated. The #1 job is psychological safety.
- The "three revolutions" framework and "voice mode is the cheat code" are the two highest-impact teaching beats.
- Don't over-teach features. Install mindsets.
- Cover data security properly in this session (full slide, real discussion). Sessions 2-5 get one-sentence reminders only.
- The $20 assistant vs. $700 advisor frame plants the seed for everything that follows.

## Session 2: Prompting & Model Selection

- Prompting 101/201/301 is the framework. The "bark was rough" live-edit moment (having a student's prompt fail, then fixing it together) is the single best teaching beat.
- The magic sentence ("ask me clarifying questions, one at a time, up to ___") is consistently the most-referenced takeaway across all cohorts.
- Session 2 tends to run dense. 22+ concepts is too many. Pick 3-4 anchor concepts and hold the line.
- Model selection teaching ("manually pick the thinking model, don't let auto decide") needs to land early and exactly once.
- The Patty-style live-edit demo (take a student's prompt, edit it on screen, show the output shift) is worth protecting at all costs.

## Session 3: Creative Content Generation

- "One idea, multiple outputs" is the session's thesis. The workflow (Think > Frame > Structure > Design > Ship) is Danoosh's proprietary framing.
- The cooking metaphor for prompt chaining ("you don't start at the stove") has replaced the IKEA metaphor. It's stickier because it models adaptive judgment, not linear instructions. Use the clean 4-panel editorial illustration (chef thinking > prep > tasting > plated dish).
- Image prompting is taught INSIDE the workflow demo, not as a separate section. The vague-vs-detailed side-by-side contrast is Wow Moment 2. Pre-generate both images before class to eliminate live-generation risk.
- Cut the Book Haven independent activity. The micro-activity (write one flyer headline, drop in Slack) is enough.
- AI roles slide: keep but compress to 60 seconds.
- Remove proprietary info slide: one sentence reminder, not a full slide.
- Poll on content types: cut. Doesn't change what you teach.
- The Afterparty is where Danoosh shows real client artifacts (proposals, brand design, Notion systems). This is the highest-value conversion asset across all 5 sessions.

## Session 4: Custom GPTs & Projects

- The icebreaker "ask ChatGPT what tasks it sees you repeating" consistently generates strong engagement and directly seeds the Custom GPT teaching. Refined version: "Based on everything you know about me from our conversations so far, what are 2-3 tasks I keep coming back to you for that could be turned into a Custom GPT?"
- The Custom GPT vs. Project vs. Skill File distinction is confusing for students. Use concrete examples:
  - GPT = job postings, RFP evaluation, permit review (same output every time)
  - Project = home remodel, client pipeline, candidate hiring (long-running, multiple threads)
  - Skill File = automated coaching follow-up, evening cleanup agent (full workflow automation)
- The live GPT build demo should use something relatable (marketing copywriter "Clara," not a niche use case).
- The "must click the black Update button" warning is critical. Every cohort has someone lose an hour of work.
- Don't introduce Skill Files in the main session. Save for Afterparty as an "advanced preview."
- Gemini Gems: mention in 60 seconds, don't demo. Most students are on ChatGPT.
- Afterparty: skill file demo (post-coaching follow-up workflow) + Notion Second Brain system. This is where the sophistication gap becomes visible. Also showed evening cleanup agent and morning briefing agent.

## Session 5: Data Analysis + Course Close

- This is the final session. It serves three functions: teach data analysis, create the transformation moment, and scatter breadcrumbs for lead gen.
- Cut AI news section unless something major dropped. Redistribute time to reflection.
- Data analysis demo should use relatable data (personal finance CSV), not exec-level business scenarios. The Jan/Feb cohort's decision-making demo was strong substance but too strategic for rank-and-file students. The workflow is the same; the data should be accessible.
- The demo sequence: orient > analyze trends > visualize (charts) > pivot to decision-making > adapt for audience. Five beats, each building on the last.
- The "data as the new oil" mental model: broaden what students think of as data. Inbox, social media, transcripts, screenshots. AI is the refinery.
- Two cheat-sheet slides for the close:
  - "Your AI Starter Kit" (5 tactical things to do this week)
  - "Level Up Habits" (5 things that compound over time)
- The reflection close ("What will you be brave enough to attempt?") is the most important 10 minutes of the entire cohort. Don't rush it. Every response is a potential testimonial. Place it AFTER the data demo, not before.
- Drop LinkedIn/email/website in chat during the close. Frame as "staying connected."
- Afterparty is shorter (15-20 min if Danoosh has evening commitments). Open Q&A works best. Let the five weeks of watching him work be the pitch.
- The Jan/Feb Session 5 felt like a failure to Danoosh but was actually strong. Joy said it "changed my approach to how I see the world." Austin built an HR compliance matrix for 48 states in 30 minutes. Multiple students reported 20+ colleagues signing up. RSD misread cognitive stretch as failure.
