# Decision Brief Template Reference

## When to Produce This

Only when the diagnostic session included a live decision-framing exercise. Look for evidence in the transcript of:
- The Framing Stack (5 questions on pen and paper)
- Scenario modeling (best/base/worst case)
- Whiteboard or visual artifact creation
- A specific decision being worked through with AI

If the session was exploratory, tool-focused, or didn't involve working a specific decision, skip this deliverable entirely.

## Document Sections (in order)

### Cover Page
- DK Consulting logo (top-left, ~90x76px)
- Title: "DECISION BRIEF" (Helvetica Bold, 28pt, black, stacked)
- Khaki divider line below title
- Subtitle: "[DECISION TOPIC IN CAPS]" (Helvetica Bold, 10pt, khaki, can be 2 lines)
- Bottom block: "Prepared for [Prospect Full Name]" / Danoosh Kapadia, AI Educator & Strategist / hello@danooshkapadia.com / [Date]

### 01 — THE DECISION AS IT STANDS TODAY
Open with a framing paragraph: "This report summarizes the decision conversation from our working session on [date]. The goal is not to make the decision for you. The goal is to help you see it more clearly, test your assumptions, and identify the next steps."

Then two labeled sections:
- **The decision now:** One sentence framing the actual decision on the table.
- **Not deciding yet:** Short paragraph listing what is explicitly NOT being decided at this stage. This is critical because it reduces the perceived weight of the decision and makes action feel safer.

Close with: "This distinction matters. [Why the narrower frame is more useful.]"

### 02 — WHAT PROBLEM YOU ARE ACTUALLY TRYING TO SOLVE
Name what the real problem is, explicitly noting what it is NOT (common misconception). Use bullet list for the specific symptoms, then a callout box with the reframed question.

The callout box question format: "How do we [actual goal] without [key risk or constraint]?"

### 03 — WHAT WINNING LOOKS LIKE
"You defined success in practical terms during the session. A stronger and fuller version of winning, within [timeframe], would look like this:"

Bullet list of 5-7 concrete, measurable outcomes.

Close with a line about what the definition keeps the focus on.

### 04 — WHAT EMERGED FROM THE CONVERSATION
Series of bold-labeled paragraphs, each surfacing a key insight from the Q&A exchange during the session. Typical labels:
- The primary objective is mixed.
- The bottleneck is [X], not [Y].
- The bigger risk is not trying.
- The preferred first move is clear.
- The [key concern] is real, but not disqualifying.

Each paragraph should show the reasoning, not just the conclusion. "When asked [question], your answer was [answer]. That indicates [insight]."

### 05 — THE DECISION, STATED CLEANLY
One sentence in a callout box. This is the reframed decision after all the conversation work.

Follow with: "This framing is stronger than [original framing]. It is more accurate, less emotionally loaded, and more consistent with the actual business problem."

### 06 — WHAT YOU MAY NOT BE FULLY CONSIDERING
Series of bold-labeled paragraphs surfacing blind spots. Each follows the pattern:
- Name the blind spot
- Explain why it matters
- Describe the risk if it goes unaddressed
- Do NOT prescribe a solution (that's the prospect's job)

Typical categories: operating model vs. headcount, concentration risk, management support gaps, relationship management, recruiting as positioning.

### 07 — HOW THIS PLAYS OUT OVER TIME
Data table: Scenario (Best/Base/Worst) × Time Horizons (6 months / 18 months / 3 years)

Follow with: "The biggest variables that determine which scenario becomes real are: [list 5-6 key variables]."

If a whiteboard sketch or visual artifact was created during the session, embed it as a centered image below the table. Use the original image file from uploads.

### 08 — THE HUMAN SIDE OF THE DECISION
Address the emotional and reputational layer directly. Name the concern the prospect voiced about what happens if this goes wrong.

Validate the concern. Validate their instincts. Then offer the stronger executive posture in a callout box.

Format: "A strong posture here is not: '[weak version].' A stronger posture is: '[strong version].'"

Close with: "That is not hesitation. It is sound judgment."

### 09 — WHAT APPEARS TRUE RIGHT NOW
Two labeled lists:

**Likely true:** Bullet list of 5-7 things that appear credible based on the discussion.

**Still uncertain:** Bullet list of 4-6 things that remain unresolved and would benefit from more evidence.

### 10 — TAKING THIS FURTHER ON YOUR OWN
One concise section that points the prospect to working with their AI independently. NOT a pitch for more Danoosh time.

Structure:
1. Short paragraph: "The framing work from our session gave you a structured starting point. To move toward a higher-confidence recommendation, the next step is to bring real data into the conversation with your AI."
2. One paragraph listing the most useful data inputs (process docs, throughput data, performance data, org structure, etc.)
3. Three concepts explained in plain language (2-3 sentences each):
   - **Define the field:** Surface hidden assumptions, constraints, missing options, alternative frames.
   - **Run a future walkthrough:** Model scenarios with real data, including what would need to be true for each path.
   - **Complete a second-order scan:** What gets locked in, what becomes hard to unwind, what this trains the organization to become.
4. Closing paragraph: "After your AI has done its work, the final calls remain yours." List the key questions (trade-offs, values at stake, 7-day next action, what would change your mind).

### 11 — CLOSING PERSPECTIVE
Two to three paragraphs synthesizing the overall picture. No new information. Just perspective.

**Final line:** Bold and italic. One memorable sentence that the prospect will remember and could repeat to others. This is the signature line of the document.

## Technical Production Notes

Same brand standards and production process as the SOW template. Key differences:
- Header reads "DK CONSULTING | DECISION BRIEF" (not "AI COACHING PROGRAM")
- Cover subtitle is the decision topic in khaki caps (not "PREPARED FOR [NAME]")
- "Prepared for [Name]" moves to the bottom block
- Callout boxes used more frequently (reframed questions, executive posture)
- Scenario table uses 4-column layout: Label (1200) + 6mo (2053) + 18mo (2053) + 3yr (2054) = 7360
- Embedded images should be centered, ~520px wide, with breathing room above and below
