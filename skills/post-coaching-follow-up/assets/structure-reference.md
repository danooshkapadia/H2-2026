# Client Follow-Up Note: Structure Reference

Load this when drafting the client follow-up in Step 9.

## Body structure (7 sections)

### 1. Warm opener that names the meta-shift

Not a greeting platitude. Name what was actually different about this session at the altitude level.

**Good:**
- *"You're doing something most people say they want with AI, but very few actually do: you're using it to build real leverage without outsourcing your judgment."*
- *"The big shift I saw today was you moving from thinking about AI as a tool to thinking about it as a thinking partner."*

**Bad:**
- *"Great session today!"*
- *"Thanks for the time today."*

2 to 4 sentences. Should make the client feel seen.

### 2. The insight layer

Pick the pattern that fits:

**Pattern A — "Three moments that mattered"**
Name 3 specific moments in the session and interpret their meaning. What shifted, why it mattered, what it unlocks.

**Pattern B — "The big shift I saw"**
One core meta-observation, followed by 2 to 4 specific takeaways that support it.

Each insight should **interpret meaning, not report content.** Don't summarize what happened. Name what it means.

### 3. The lock-in layer

Short section titled something like "A few ideas to lock in" or "Key takeaways." 3 to 5 crisp principles or frameworks the client can hold onto. These are the stickiness hooks.

Each is one line: a bolded key phrase followed by a brief explanation.

### 4. Homework (small, specific, repeatable)

2 to 3 items max. Each concrete, time-bound, low-friction. Label them clearly with a sub-header and a short context line, then 2 to 3 specific steps as bullets.

### 5. What's next

One short paragraph (2 to 3 sentences) on what the next session will cover, framed as a natural continuation of what just opened up.

### 6. Session Resources

Always include:
- **Fathom recording link**
- **Gamma deck link** (if there's one)
- **Any other artifacts** (Google Docs, PDFs, visuals, prompts, templates)

Format as bullets with a bolded label and a link.

### 7. Warm close

One line reinforcing progress or the compounding nature of the work.

Then on a new line: *"Looking forward to seeing you at our next session on {Month} {Day}{ordinal}."* (e.g., "Looking forward to seeing you at our next session on Apr 12th.")

Sign off: "Warmly," or "Talk soon," — match Danoosh's recent pattern for that client if you can see it.

---

## Voice principles

- **Strategic and concise.** No fluff. Every line links back to purpose or learning.
- **Insight-anchored.** Interpret meaning, don't report content.
- **Warm, human, editor-not-marketer.** Clean, calm, grounded. Quietly confident.
- **No hype, no tech-bro framing, no false binaries.** Nuance over contrast.
- **Speak to the underlying psychology.** Use the psych doc to shape the emotional register. What does this client actually care about? What are they afraid of? What do they hope this is unlocking?

## Tone modulators

- **Analytical client** → lead with frameworks, structured reasoning, explicit trade-offs, decision architecture. Name the logic.
- **Intuitive/emotional client** → lead with awareness, resonance, pattern recognition, what they're sensing. Name the felt shift.

Most clients are a blend. Weight the modulators based on what the psych doc says and what you saw in the transcript.

## Formatting rules

- Limit em dashes. Use commas, periods, or colons.
- Bold key phrases inline sparingly — skimmer reading only the bold should get the core ideas.
- Use bullets where they help, prose where it flows better. Don't over-structure.
- Alternate rhythms: narrative paragraph → structured section → narrative → structured.
