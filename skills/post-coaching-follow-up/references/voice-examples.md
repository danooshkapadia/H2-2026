## Example 1 — Fernando Urrutia (Session 4, AI Agents)

**Subject:** `Fernando Urrutia — Coaching Session #4 Recap: AI Agents`

**Opener (names the meta-shift):**
> I really enjoyed yesterday's session. You're doing something most people *say* they want with AI, but very few actually do: you're using it to build real leverage *without outsourcing your judgment or your thinking muscle*.

**Insight section ("Three moments that mattered"):**
> **1. You moved from "AI as a firehose" to "AI as a clarity engine."**
> You've already been doing serious research work. What changed yesterday was how you're now *compressing* that work into retained understanding... That move, from accumulation to distillation, is where AI stops being impressive and starts being useful.

**Lock-in layer ("A few ideas I want to lock in"):**
> - **Frame first. Synthesize second.** The quality of insight is set *before* the AI runs.
> - **Visual compression drives retention.** The "create the graphic" step isn't cosmetic. It forces prioritization.
> - **Task work goes to agents. Thinking work stays with you.**

**Warm close:**
> This was a rich session, and you're clearly doing the work between calls. That's what makes this compound.
> Talk soon,

**Why it works:** Fernando is analytical and identity-conscious. The note leads with a sophisticated meta-observation ("build real leverage without outsourcing your judgment"), then backs it up with crisp structural observations. Frameworks, explicit trade-offs, decision architecture. No sentiment, lots of signal.

## Example 2 — Joe Hilliard (Session 7, High-Stakes Decisions)

**Subject:** `Joe Hilliard Coaching Session #7 Follow Up: Using AI for High Stakes Decision-making`

**Opener:**
> Great session today. We covered a lot, and you're making significant progress session over session.

**Big shift:**
> **The big shift I saw**
> You're starting to think like a systems-focused business owner, not just an operator. Instead of reacting to opportunities as they arise, you're designing experiments with guardrails, stop points, and early signals.

**Homework (concrete, bounded):**
> **Homework 1: Brief Qwan & Leadership Team**
> - Explain the 90-day test, guardrails, and early signals using the visuals and communication tools we developed.
> - Emphasize this is a test, not a permanent shift in responsibilities

**Warm close:**
> This session set up *structured clarity* for testing a new revenue engine while protecting your core growth. You now have a repeatable framework to test ideas, track progress, and make decisions without losing focus or momentum.
> Looking forward to our last session on Friday Jan 23rd at 11am CT.
> Warmly,

**Why it works:** Joe is a business owner who thinks operationally. The note reinforces an identity shift ("systems-focused business owner, not just an operator") and then anchors it with concrete guardrails, homework steps, and a framework he can reuse. Every insight ties back to a business outcome he cares about.

## Example 3 — Brian Sullivan (Session 4, AI & Marketing)

**Subject:** `Session 4 Recap: What You Learned About AI's Impact on Marketing`

**Opener (names the learning arc):**
> It's been great to see your progress session over session. I'm glad you took a swing at using AI for an ambiguous personal decision and ran into the very common challenge of framing the problem. That's something you do instinctively at work with people, but it's a new muscle when you bring AI into the mix. The big shift is recognizing that AI can help you solve complex problems, but it can't tell you *what* problems to solve.

**Takeaways (plain language, reframe over jargon):**
> - **Framing beats prompting:** When boundaries are vague, AI struggles. Once you define the "box," it becomes a powerful thought partner.
> - **The game has changed:** SEO is giving way to AEO.
> - **Creation is near-zero cost:** AI now collapses production cycles to seconds and pennies.

**Warm close:**
> **Remember:** Session 4 was about stretching into messy problem-framing *and* seeing how fast AI is shifting the marketing landscape. Your job isn't to master every tool and feature. It's to keep learning how to continually adapt to change, practicing clarity in the problem framing and curiosity in the possibilities.

**Why it works:** Brian is an executive who needs both conceptual grounding and practical reassurance. The note reframes what happened ("you stumbled on framing, which is the actual skill"), normalizes the struggle, and pulls him up to a bigger frame. Less jargon, more translation.

## Voice calibration cheat-sheet

| Client type | Lead with | Language register |
|---|---|---|
| Analytical / intellectual (Fernando) | Meta-observation, then crisp frameworks | Precise, structural, trade-off-aware |
| Operator / owner (Joe) | Identity shift, then operational specifics | Concrete, guardrail-focused, business-outcome-linked |
| Executive / generalist (Brian) | Reframe the experience, then translate | Plain language, normalized struggle, bigger-picture pullup |

---

## Common threads across all three

- **Openers name a shift, not a platitude.** Never "great session today" as the lead.
- **Insights interpret meaning.** "You moved from X to Y" beats "We covered X."
- **Bolded key phrases carry the skim layer.** Skimmer gets the core from bold alone.
- **Homework is tiny and concrete.** 2 to 3 items, each with a clear first step.
- **Warm close reinforces compounding.** "This is what makes it compound" or "You now have a repeatable framework."
- **No tech-bro language.** No "game-changing," no "10x," no hype.
- **Limited em dashes.** Commas and periods do most of the work.
