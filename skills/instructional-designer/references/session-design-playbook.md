# Session Design Playbook

The deep delivery patterns, generalized from real programs (CFI LEAD, CFI ELT, Rockline, Amada, Ziplines). Read at Phase 3 for any live session design.

## Start from the insight, not the template

The whole design hangs off one discovery insight, stated as a sentence. Examples of the caliber required: "They already do advanced things with AI; they just lack the shared language to be deliberate, consistent, or teach each other." Find that sentence before designing. If the design could have been produced without the research, it's a template, not a design.

## The experience arc (emotional shape first)

Write the arc before the agenda. A proven arc for a build-centered session:

1. **Pride and proof** (open) — show the climb so far; status and momentum.
2. **Desire** (demo) — one short, real demo from THEIR world. They should think "I want that."
3. **Agency** (build round 1) — everyone makes a thing, lockstep. "I made it. It's mine."
4. **The wow** (the engineered moment) — the visceral beat that proves this is a different kind of tool.
5. **Ownership and stretch** (build round 2) — apply the same moves to higher-value work of their choosing; the room self-levels.
6. **Transformation** (share-out) — someone's build works on their real world, in front of everyone. Social proof from inside their own walls.
7. **Meaning and proof** (close) — celebrate movement, capture evidence, name the road ahead.

For an orientation session (Session 1s): awe early (sponsor demo) → a designed dip, named and reframed through the facilitator's own vulnerable story → hopes and fears aired (write privately, post to chat, a few aloud; normalize every fear; do NOT solve them; the airing is the work) → steady lift into their own build.

## The two engineered moments

Design these deliberately; never hope they emerge.

- **The WOW moment.** The cleanest demonstration that this is different (scheduling an agent to run at 7am: "You did not summon it. It showed up."). One per session. Protect it from cuts.
- **The TRANSFORMATION moment.** Where "another AI thing" becomes "this changes how we work," usually social: a peer's build working on real data, or the team-level beat spoken in the sponsor's own words. This is also where the next tier of work gets seeded without a sales beat.

## Run of show rules

- Table format: time, block, minutes, what happens, energy.
- ~70% hands-on / 30% consolidate-and-close for build sessions.
- Pre-mark the cuts: which blocks shrink or die if running long. Never cut the close, the wow, or live evidence capture.
- Hard checkpoint: name the "start the centerpiece by X:XX no matter what" time.
- Every block gets an energy label. If three consecutive blocks carry the same energy, redesign.

## Dead-air engineering (Zoom hands-on)

Silent heads-down time on Zoom with a mixed room is where sessions die. Remove it four ways:

1. **Lockstep, not heads-down**, for the universal build: facilitator builds live, narrating every field continuously; the room mirrors.
2. **Deputize power users** as live chat helpers. It covers support without breakouts AND keeps the "this is too basic" voices engaged.
3. **The field guide carries the pace.** Every prompt and step written out, so falling behind live pace strands nobody.
4. **Micro-shares between steps** ("drop what you named yours in the chat"): low-pressure participation that works for the quiet ones and keeps the room alive.

Round 2 (self-paced) is where dead air creeps back: helpers circulate, the facilitator reads the next recipe aloud, beginners are told explicitly that polishing round 1 counts as complete.

## Homework math

- **In-class = what a solo participant can finish. Homework = what needs people who aren't in the room** (sharing, team collaboration) — explained in class, done in the week.
- Total load 60-90 minutes. Count what's already publicly committed before adding anything.
- At least one piece car-friendly (voice mode). It doubles as reps on the talk-to-it habit.
- Every prompt written out. Nothing to invent. "Rough beats perfect" stated explicitly.
- Never land pre-work or homework right before a holiday or break; if unavoidable, split into a warm recap (no new asks) and a post-break activation email (one ask, phone-actionable).
- Homework points at the persistent artifact. Random exercises don't compound.
- Design the follow-through: who nudges (team lead's channel post), what the catch-up path is for absentees, and how completion becomes visible without shaming (show of hands framed as celebration, wins in the channel).

## Two tracks, named once

Guided track: templates, pairing, copy-paste. Stretch track: connectors, real data, the harder build. Say both out loud once at the start so nobody feels behind or bored. Assign the stretch by name in your facilitator notes (you know who they are from research).

## The overlap trap

If the client ran a lower-tier program, that content is in the building. Inventory it, then pitch one altitude above: same framework canon is fine, but the exec version is about judgment, decision quality, and leading the change, not features. Don't over-index on your own signature content; if the room's reports already saw it, it reads as regurgitation. Audience need first, existing assets second.

## Friction tables

Every design carries two tables:

1. **Friction observed last time → fix this time.** (Prompts lost in chat → numbered in the handbook, link pinned. Copy-paste pulls formatting → one guide line + the paste trick. Embedded-app AI weak → build in the full app.)
2. **Risk → contingency.** (Access breaks → pair with a neighbor, don't stop the room. Demo data missing → synthetic scenario labeled illustrative. The fear beat goes heavy → let it breathe, validate, don't debate. Room drifts to AI philosophy → park warmly, return to building. Tenant/license uncertainty → verified fallback build.)

## Assessment integration (the ladder)

Six rungs: 0 not started · 1 lookup · 2 drafting · 3 thinking partner · 4 workflow · 5 automation · 6 team redesign. Learners see plain-English descriptions, never level numbers. Realistic movement: 1-2 rungs per program.

- **A built artifact is the strongest evidence.** Design sessions so the proof manufactures itself (everyone ships a working thing, then writes one line: "what I built and the job it does").
- **Capture surveys live in session**, framed as a victory lap, never a test. Async capture loses a third of responses.
- **Code conservatively** (ceiling demonstrated, not role or potential; when torn, the lower rung). Honest movement is what makes the story credible to a skeptical sponsor. Progress reports, never sales pitches: showing the unreached top of the ladder is what makes the next engagement self-evident.
- Recaps may reinforce ladder movement warm and aggregate ("most of you are moving from drafting into thinking partner"); never call out an individual's level.

## Tie-backs and loop-closing

- Open every session by closing the loop from the last one ("I told you we'd turn that SOP into an agent. Today's the day.").
- Answer named questions from last session by name ("Vance asked how to trust calculations you can't check. Here's the answer."). It teaches the room that speaking up pays.
- Reward visible homework publicly and specifically (three things done well + one step-up question), unattributed for fears, attributed for courage and wins.
- Feedback from session N visibly shapes session N+1, and say so: "you asked for more X, so today we're doing X."

## Sponsor integration

The sponsor opens Session 1 with their own real demo and the line "yours will be different." AI framed as an invitation to lead, not a mandate. Mid-program, the sponsor's fast-feedback ritual (last 5 minutes, every session) is the recalibration instrument. Mine every sponsor call for design inputs in their words; deliver their success criteria back as session beats.

## The working method (meta)

Brainstorm → plan → design brief → sign-off → build assets → deliver → +/delta → capture learnings. Keep a Master Design State of what's locked vs provisional (early sessions tight, later sessions gated on the midpoint). Expect several redesigns; that's the process working. Verify every stat and quote before it hits a slide, and commission the hero visual separately rather than trusting deck auto-art (one headline font, one illustration language, one red accent, no garbled AI text).
