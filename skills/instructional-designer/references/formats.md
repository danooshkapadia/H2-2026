# The Five Formats

What changes across Danoosh's teaching contexts. Read the matching section at Phase 0, every time. The spine principles apply everywhere; these are the dials.

---

## 1. 1:1 Coaching (execs and business owners)

**Audience:** one senior person, often a founder/owner or exec. High trust, high stakes, real business on the table.
**Shape:** 60 min, recurring, Zoom. The "cohort" is one person, so the session IS the relationship.
**Baseline:** varies wildly; check prior session notes and homework completion before designing.
**What changes in the design:**
- The persistent artifact is their real business system (a Project, an agent, a decision framework), built live on their actual work. No synthetic exercises, ever.
- The emotional arc is coaching-shaped: safety first, then honest challenge, then agency. Danoosh's client relationship rule: trusted advisor, never salesperson.
- Homework is one move, not three. A single well-chosen rep beats a list.
- Session design = a facilitation guide with 2-3 beats, prepared questions that make them think (not fixes that make them dependent), and a "if they arrive with a crisis, drop the plan" contingency. The plan serves the person, not the reverse.
- Openings: check what happened since last time and what they actually need today before running the plan. Closings: they state their commitment out loud; you don't state it for them.
**Pitfalls:** over-preparing content and under-preparing questions; designing for the client you wish they were rather than their actual energy that day.
**Related skills:** pre-coaching-session-prep and post-coaching-follow-up own the prep/recap workflows; this skill owns the learning design inside them.

## 2. AI Team Sprint (LEAD-style corporate cohort)

**Audience:** ~15-20 managers and doers, mixed levels (0 to 5 on the ladder), often cross-functional or cross-entity, sometimes cross-cultural. Tool is whatever the org runs (often Microsoft Copilot; feature names and menus must match THEIR tool).
**Shape:** 2-hr kickoff + five 75-min sessions, 7-10 day cadence, Teams/Slack channel between, recordings + recap emails.
**Baseline:** most start Level 1-2; realistic movement is 1-2 rungs over six weeks. Never promise the top of the ladder.
**What changes in the design:**
- Set curriculum, adapted exercises. The five-session arc: mindsets → outputs → thinking partner → communication → workflow/agents. Sessions 1-3 individual fluency, 4-5 toward team fluency.
- The range in the room is the point: two tracks always, power users deputized as chat helpers, "polishing the earlier build counts" as the floor.
- Peer learning is the engine: micro-shares, show-and-tell, one person's win posted in the channel becomes the team's knowledge. The team lead (a Carl) is the channel's engine; design their cross-post into every follow-up.
- Recaps are fuller (the audience includes people who process by reading), homework has copy-ready prompts, and the handbook/field guide carries anyone who falls behind live pace.
- Assessment: pre/mid/post on the six-rung ladder. Capture surveys LIVE in session (async completion cost 6 of 20 responses at one midpoint). A built artifact is stronger evidence than a self-report.
**Pitfalls:** designing for the advanced voices who say "too basic" and losing the bottom four; silent heads-down time on Zoom (dead air); prompts buried in chat instead of the handbook.

## 3. Executive Team Sprint (ELT-style, bespoke)

**Audience:** ~12 senior leaders (CEO, VPs, GMs, functional heads), sponsor IN the room. Typically start high Level 3. They think in P&L, respond to compression, and smell canned content instantly.
**Shape:** 2-hr Session 1 + four 75-min sessions + build session. Bespoke: every session designed from discovery findings and their real data. That bespoke-ness is what justifies the price; protect it.
**What changes in the design:**
- **The sponsor is a co-teacher.** They open, demo their own real use, frame AI as an invitation to lead rather than a mandate. Mine their words from discovery for design inputs; their success criteria are the curriculum's north star.
- **Session 1 is orientation, not provocation** (base camp). The ceiling of the room adopts at the pace of the least comfortable person.
- Bring-your-own everything: their strategy, their live decisions (from the pre-sprint survey), their staff meeting. Scaffolding and guardrails so nobody stares at a blank page, but the material is always theirs.
- The overlap trap is acute here: their managers may have already run the LEAD program. Inventory what it covered; pitch one altitude above (judgment, decision quality, leadership modeling), never re-run it.
- Exec compression everywhere: recaps 500-650 words, field guides paint-by-numbers, homework 60-90 min with a car-friendly piece, one ask per email.
- Watch tech friction like a hawk: a 15-minute add-in debug in front of a CEO is expensive. Gear checks happen between sessions, owned by IT, never inside the room or the recap.
**Pitfalls:** re-running signature content their reports already saw; pre-work landing before a holiday; unverified stats in front of finance leaders; over-provoking a mixed exec room.

## 4. AI Community Call

**Audience:** open or semi-open, mixed levels, voluntary attendance, no sponsor, no homework enforcement. People came for energy and one usable thing.
**Shape:** ~60 min, recurring. Lighter by design.
**What changes in the design:**
- One idea, one live demo, one conversation. Cut everything else. The win is "I saw something real and I can try it tonight."
- The ritual skeleton matters MORE here (it's what makes a drop-in crowd feel like a community): same opening ritual, same share-out structure, same close.
- No homework; instead "one thing to try before next time," zero guilt attached.
- Design for the lurkers: chat-first participation, low-risk shares, never cold-call.
- Demos on real, relatable work (email, meetings, a decision), not impressive edge cases.
**Pitfalls:** overstuffing; designing for the regulars and losing newcomers; letting Q&A eat the engineered close.

## 5. Ziplines exec ed

**Audience:** career-advancing professionals and career changers, paying students, evenings after work. Motivated but tired; energy management is a design constraint.
**Shape:** structured curriculum (fixed syllabus), 2-hr live online sessions, cohort model, provided materials that Danoosh adapts rather than authors.
**What changes in the design:**
- The syllabus is fixed; the experience is yours. Design freedom lives in openings, transitions, exercises, examples, and the emotional arc, not the topic list.
- Evening energy: front-load interaction, alternate teach/do every 10-15 minutes, use movement and voice, never lecture past minute 12.
- Wins wall: start with participant wins from the week (their material is the social proof and the retention engine).
- Their careers are the stakes: tie every technique to a job-market or on-the-job outcome. "This is the thing you'll show in an interview."
- Follow the provider's structures where required (their LMS, their feedback forms) and layer the ritual skeleton inside those constraints.
**Pitfalls:** treating provided slides as the session (they're the floor, not the design); pacing for the strongest students at 8pm when the median is fading.
