# Slide Bullets Reference — For Gamma Decks

Load this when producing Artifact 3 in Step 6.

## Purpose

These are the on-screen content bullets Danoosh pastes into Gamma to generate a coaching session deck. They are not speaker notes. They are not a script. They are what the client reads on screen while Danoosh talks.

## Voice rules for slide bullets

Slide bullets are written to the client's psychology doc. The language register, metaphor world, and cognitive level must match the person sitting across the Zoom.

**For a doer/craftsperson client (e.g., Fabrizio):**
- Use physical, tangible language. "Build," "set up," "organize," "connect."
- Use metaphors from their world. Kitchen stations, ingredients, recipes, tools.
- Short, declarative. One idea per bullet. No compound sentences.
- No jargon. If a technical term is necessary, follow it with a plain-language restatement on the same line.
- Never assume fluency in English idioms. Write for clarity, not cleverness.

**For an analytical/executive client (e.g., Fernando):**
- Use framework language. "Model," "structure," "layer," "sequence."
- Bullets can carry more conceptual weight. Trade-offs, decision architecture, system design.
- Precision matters more than warmth on slides. Save warmth for the conversation.

**For a generalist/explorer client (e.g., Brian):**
- Lead with the reframe, not the definition. "AI doesn't replace your judgment. It gives you better inputs."
- Normalize the learning curve. "This is where most people get stuck."
- Bullets should translate, not teach. Big idea first, mechanism second.

## Bullet format rules

- **One line per bullet.** Not a fragment. Not a paragraph. One clear, complete thought that earns its place on screen.
- **Explanatory, not terse.** A bullet should make sense to the client if they screenshot the slide and look at it tomorrow without Danoosh's narration. "Context is king" is too terse. "The more context you give AI about your situation, the better it performs" is right.
- **Not too long either.** If a bullet wraps to three lines on a slide, it's too long. Target 8–18 words per bullet.
- **No sub-bullets on slides.** If you need hierarchy, use a new slide.
- **Bold key phrases sparingly.** One bold phrase per slide max, for the anchor idea.

## Slide structure (typical coaching deck, 5–8 slides)

```
SLIDE 1 — Title
- Session [N]: [Topic Name]
- [Client First Name] + Danoosh · [Date]

SLIDE 2 — Where we are
- [One bullet recapping last session's key shift]
- [One bullet on what's changed since then / homework signal]

SLIDE 3 — Today's concept
- [The concept in one sentence, written to the client's level]
- [Why this matters for YOUR business / life]
- [The analogy or frame that makes it land]

SLIDE 4 — How it works (optional, for concept-heavy sessions)
- [Step 1 of the concept]
- [Step 2 of the concept]
- [Step 3 of the concept]
- Keep to 3–4 bullets max. If more, split into two slides.

SLIDE 5 — What we're building today
- [One sentence describing the artifact / workflow / tool]
- [What it will do for you once it's built]
- [What you'll need: bring X, open Y, have Z ready]

SLIDE 6 — The build (optional, for complex builds)
- [Step-by-step of what we'll do together]
- Only if the build has distinct phases worth previewing

SLIDE 7 — Homework
- [Item 1: what + why, one line]
- [Item 2: what + why, one line]
- [Stretch goal, clearly marked optional]

SLIDE 8 — What's next
- Next session: [Date]
- [One line previewing the next session's topic and why it follows naturally]
```

Adjust the count up or down based on the session. A build-heavy session might only need 4 slides (title, concept, what we're building, homework). A concept-heavy session might need 7–8.

## What NOT to do on slides

- No walls of text. If a slide has more than 5 bullets, split it.
- No clip art, stock icons, or decorative elements. Gamma will handle visual design.
- No speaker notes in the bullets. Those belong in the facilitation guide.
- No questions to the client on slides. Questions are verbal. Slides are statements.
- No "agenda" slide with a list of 6 topics. That signals overload, which is the opposite of "one concept, one build."
- No jargon the client would have to look up. If you write "SOP" for a client who hasn't learned that term yet, write "Standard Operating Procedure (SOP), a step-by-step guide your team can follow."
