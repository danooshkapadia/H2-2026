# Coaching Facilitation Guide — Structure Reference

Load this when producing Artifact 1 in Step 6.

## Purpose

This document is for Danoosh to read before the session. It helps him internalize the arc, the timing, the key coaching moves, and the internal notes that guide his instincts during the live session. It is never shared with the client.

Think of it as the director's annotated script. The client sees the performance. Danoosh sees the stage directions.

## Structure

### Header block

```
# Session [N] — [Client First Name] + Danoosh
[Day], [Date] · [Time] PT · Zoom

---
```

### Welcome / opening frame (2–4 sentences)

A brief internal note on how to open the session. Not a script. A reminder of the emotional tone and the first question to ask. Usually starts with checking in on how things have been since last session, what they tried, what they noticed.

**Internal coaching note format:** When you need to give Danoosh a coaching note (something about how to handle a moment, what to watch for, what to avoid), use this format:

```
> **Internal:** [Note for Danoosh. This is about coaching craft, not content.
> The client never sees this. Be direct.]
```

### Part 1 — Check-in and homework review (10–15 min)

Structure the homework review with specific questions to ask and what to listen for. Include:

- Each homework item from the previous session
- What "done well" looks like for each (so Danoosh can recognize and name progress)
- What "not done" tells you (without judgment, what does it signal about where the client is?)
- Transition sentence to Part 2

**Internal notes** should flag: what thread to pull if the client opens up, what to leave alone, when to move on even if the conversation is interesting.

### Part 2 — The concept (10–15 min)

The one concept being taught. Structure it as:

- **The concept in one sentence** (what Danoosh says to introduce it)
- **The analogy or frame** (how to make it land for this specific client, using their world, their language, their metaphors)
- **The "why this matters for you" bridge** (connecting the concept to their stated need or deeper arc)
- **The check** (one question to confirm they got it before moving to the build)

Keep this section tight. Fabrizio-type clients (doers, craftspeople, ESL) need 5 minutes of explanation and 40 minutes of building. Fernando-type clients (analytical, frameworks-oriented) can handle 15 minutes of concept before building.

**Internal notes** should flag: where to expect confusion, what not to over-explain, when to stop talking and start doing.

### Part 3 — The build (30–40 min)

The heart of the session. Structure it as a step-by-step walkthrough of what they're building together. For each step:

- **What happens:** The action (e.g., "Open ChatGPT. Start a new project called 'Operations.'")
- **Who drives:** Whether Danoosh is driving (demo) or the client is driving (coached practice). Alternate. Client should drive at least 50% of the build.
- **What to say:** Key framing sentences, not a script. Bullet points Danoosh can glance at.
- **What to watch for:** Common mistakes, moments where the client might get stuck, where to intervene vs. let them struggle.

**Internal notes** should flag: where scope creep is likely ("can it also do X?"), where to hold the boundary between coaching and implementation, and where a moment of delight is likely (name it so Danoosh can let it breathe instead of rushing past it).

### Part 4 — Reflection and close (10 min)

- **Mirror the shift:** One sentence Danoosh can say that names what changed in the session. Not what was covered. What shifted.
- **Homework introduction:** 2–3 items, each small, concrete, and repeatable. Frame each one with why it matters, not just what to do.
- **Three takeaways prompt:** Ask the client to name their top three takeaways. This forces retrieval and locks learning.
- **Feedback prompt:** Ask for feedback on how the session worked for them. ("Anything you want me to do differently?")
- **Calendar confirmation:** Confirm the next session date and time.

### Timing guide

Include a simple timing bar at the top or bottom:

```
[Check-in: 15 min] → [Concept: 10 min] → [Build: 35 min] → [Close: 15 min] = 75 min
```

Adjust proportions based on the session. Some sessions are build-heavy (40+ min build). Some are concept-heavy (when introducing a new mental model). The timing guide is a guardrail, not a constraint.

## Formatting rules

- Use markdown headers and bullet points for scannability
- Bold key phrases Danoosh needs to find quickly
- Keep internal notes visually distinct (blockquote format)
- Total length: 2–4 pages. Enough to internalize, short enough to read in 10 minutes before the session.
- No jargon the client wouldn't understand (even though this is internal, it keeps Danoosh in the right register)
- Use the client's actual phrases from prior sessions where possible
