## 1. Why it exists — four stakeholders, strict priority order

1. **Sponsor ROI proof** — the buyer must see the program was worth it, in a way that opens Phase 2 without selling. Highest priority.
2. **Individual learner growth visibility** — each participant sees their own progress and owns it.
3. **Content design input** — the pre-survey tunes session content (which workflows to feature, archetype mix, pacing).
4. **Team-lead accountability** — concrete data to manage participation with ("three people haven't engaged with the last two challenges — let's check in"). Accountability without surveillance.

**The most important posture decision:** every report is a **progress report, not a sales document**. If it reads like marketing, the trust that earns the next engagement evaporates.

## 2. Design principles (LOCKED)

- **Behavior beats confidence.** Every confidence item is subordinate to a behavioral item. Self-report inflates unpredictably (Dunning-Kruger — and AI use itself distorts metacognitive accuracy).
- **Free-response coding is ground truth.** The coded answer (against the 6-level rubric) is the truth check on self-report.
- **Questions are identical across all clients, forever.** Customization touches only headers, intro opening line, and signature. The question bank is a contract with the future benchmark product (Gallup/CultureAmp/Section all built moats this way).
- **No statistical-significance language with small cohorts.** N=12–20: use medians, ranges, individual trajectories, within-person change (paired analyses). Never "statistically significant."
- **Honest gaps build credibility.** The "where we still have work" section must have real teeth. A 100% positive report reads as sales.
- **6-point scales, no neutral middle.** Forces directional commitment.
- **4-week recall windows** for behavioral questions — long enough for habit, short enough for honest recall.
- **Manager expectation is the single biggest predictor of proficiency (2.6x, Section 2026)** — one question per touchpoint, drawn from TAM/UTAUT lineage.
- **Anonymity:** named internally (individually trackable by Danoosh + team lead); aggregate-only in sponsor reports. Participants are told this.
- **Voice:** warm, conversational, IDEO-esque — a trainer taking a baseline, obviously in service of the participant.
- **Realistic 6-week movement:** Levels 1–2 → Levels 3–4. Level 5 sightings are a win. Level 6 is Phase 2 territory — showing the unreached top of the ladder is what makes Phase 2 self-evident.

## 3. The four touchpoints

| Touchpoint | Length | Purpose |
|------------|--------|---------|
| **Pre-Sprint** | 15 q · 10–12 min | Baseline + content-design input. Heaviest. |
| **Mid-Sprint pulse** | 8 q · ~5 min | Light pulse for the team lead; fires AFTER SESSION 3. |
| **Post-Sprint** | 14 q · 8–10 min | Full measurement + outcome capture + testimonial material ("single most useful thing" question). |
| **60-day pulse** | 5 q · 3–4 min | Client-administered (team lead sends, results shared back). Sustainment check — the real durability signal, discussed live in the sponsor readout. |

Question mix per touchpoint: 75–80% multiple choice, 20–25% free response.

## 4. Three data streams

- **Stream A — Self-reported behavioral frequency.** Six questions, one per ladder level, same six every touchpoint, locked. Primary quantitative signal.
- **Stream B — Confidence by competency.** Four questions mapped to Anthropic's 4D (Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence) in plain English. Corroborating signal only.
- **Stream C — Coded free response.** "Describe one specific thing you did with AI that mattered" — coded by Claude against the 6-level rubric. The behavioral truth check. A retrospective pre-post question at post-Sprint catches inflated baselines (response-shift bias).

## 5. The Pre-Sprint Instrument v1.0 (LOCKED — full text)

**Master intro (locked):**

> **Before we begin.**
> Six weeks is just long enough to see real change. But you can only see it if you know where you started.
> So these questions are your baseline. We'll ask them again at the end. Not to grade anyone. Just so you can look back and feel proud of the progress you've made.
> The questions are easy. Some you'll answer in a second. A few ask you to write a sentence or two. The whole thing takes about ten minutes.
> My only ask is that you answer honestly, not optimistically. The Sprint is built around where this team actually is, and the more real your answers, the more useful I can make the program for you.

**Section 1 — Where you're starting (6 behavioral frequency questions).** Scale for all: Never / Once or twice / A few times a month / Once a week / A few times a week / Daily or almost daily.

1. In the past four weeks, how often have you used AI to look up information, get quick answers, or do basic research?
2. In the past four weeks, how often have you used AI to help you write something? (emails, reports, summaries, documents)
3. In the past four weeks, how often have you used AI to help you work through a problem, analyze a situation, or think through a decision?
4. In the past four weeks, how often have you used AI across multiple steps of a single task? (for example: analyzing data, then drafting a summary, then preparing the deliverable)
5. In the past four weeks, how often have you built or used a custom AI tool that handles a recurring task for you? (custom GPTs, agents, templated prompts, skill files)
6. In the past four weeks, how often have you and your team used AI together to change how a shared process or workflow gets done?

**Section 2 — How AI feels to you (4 confidence questions, 6-point anchored scales).**

7. How clear are you on what AI is genuinely good at versus where it falls short? (Not clear at all → Completely clear)
8. How confident are you in your ability to get genuinely useful output from AI? (Not at all confident → Completely confident)
9. How well do you know when to trust AI's output and when to push back on it? (I don't really know → Strong instinct for AI's blind spots)
10. How comfortable are you using AI for work that actually matters? (I avoid it → Integrated into how I work)

**Section 3 — Self-placement (1 question).**

11. Which of these best describes how you're using AI in your work right now?
- I'm not really using AI yet, or I've tried it but it didn't stick
- I use AI mostly to look up information or get quick answers
- I use AI to help me write things — emails, reports, summaries
- I use AI as a thinking partner — to work through decisions, problems, or analysis
- I use AI across whole workflows — taking something from idea to finished deliverable
- I've built reusable AI tools for work I do regularly — and they save me real time
- My team and I have changed how we work together using AI

**Section 4 — Context (1 question).**

12. How would you describe what your manager and your peers expect from you around AI?
- My manager and peers don't really care whether I use AI
- It's encouraged but not really expected
- It's expected but no one talks about it
- It's expected and we talk about it sometimes
- It's part of how we work and we share what we're learning
- We actively coordinate as a team around how we use AI

**Section 5 — In your own words (3 free-response questions).**

13. Tell me about one specific thing you did with AI in the past month that actually mattered. *(Walk me through what you were trying to do, how you used AI, and what came out of it. If you didn't use AI for anything that mattered, that's a legitimate answer — just say so.)*
14. What's the biggest bottleneck in your work right now? (Where do you lose the most time, get stuck, or feel like you're doing work the hard way?) *(Be specific.)*
15. What would make this Sprint feel worth your time when it's over? *(What would you need to be able to do, or how would your work need to feel different, for you to say "that was worth it"?)*

## 6. The coding rubric (free responses → ladder level)

Code each free response to ONE level. Multiple examples → **the highest-level example wins** (the ceiling is what they're capable of). Individual placement per touchpoint = modal level of their coded responses, stretch noted. Team placement = median of individuals.

| Level | What earns it | Example answers |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|
| 1 Lookup | Information retrieval, fact-checking, definitions, quick research | "I asked it what the federal weight limit was." |
| 2 Drafting | Text output replacing typing; single-shot writing (incl. voice/dictation) | "I had it write a customer email." "I dictated a report and had it clean it up." |
| 3 Thinking partner | Analyze, evaluate, problem-solve, pressure-test; iterative not single-shot | "I worked through a pricing decision with it." "It helped me figure out why our scrap rate was up." |
| 4 Workflow | Multiple steps toward a real outcome; analysis → communication → deliverable | "It analyzed Q1, built a deck, then wrote the talking points." |
| 5 Automation | Built/used a reusable tool, agent, custom GPT, skill file; recurring use | "I built a custom GPT for weekly status reports." |
| 6 Team redesign | Team collectively changed how work gets done; shared workflows, peer learning | "Our team built a shared AI process for our weekly leadership meeting." |

## 7. The report artifacts (same data, three deliverables)

**Sponsor Report — 5-page premium PDF** (primary; optimized for the post-Sprint readout call; follows brand-guidelines.md, 12pt minimum text):
1. The headline page — one big number, one sentence the sponsor can repeat to their CFO, set in white space.
2. What shifted — pre/post movement + anonymized individual trajectories ("Learner A: Level 2 → 4").
3. In their own words — 3–5 anonymized quotes.
4. The work they're doing now — behaviors and use cases that didn't exist eight weeks ago + task-anchored time savings.
5. Where we still have work — honest gaps (Levels 5–6 largely unreached — the natural Phase 2 territory).
6. What I'd recommend next — honest; sometimes "no Phase 2 yet, consolidate for three months." That honesty is what makes the recommendation credible when it does include more work.

**Individual Participant Summary** — 1 page per learner, their own data, given back after post-Sprint.

**Team-Lead Operational Brief** — 1–2 pages, working document: completion data, engagement signals, who's ahead/behind. Not premium.

## 9. Time-savings framing (careful)

Task-anchored questions (drafting, presentations, analysis, recurring work, research) with bounded scales (Not using / <30 min / 30–60 min / 1–2 hr / 2–4 hr / >4 hr per week). **Post-Sprint only** — pre would prime, mid is too early. Report as *"self-reported time savings on specific tasks"* — never validated productivity gains (the UK DBT Copilot evaluation found exactly this gap). The honesty about what we can't claim is what gives the rest credibility.

## 10. The benchmarking moat

Identical locked questions from cohort one onward → aggregate anonymized pre/mid/post across all Sprints → a normative dataset on mid-market AI adoption nobody else has. It becomes: the closing asset ("here's where your team starts vs. the benchmark") · the thought-leadership engine ("we measured N teams") · the authority position. Protect the question bank like the asset it is.

## 11. The Compound Step (run after EVERY checkpoint)

Each checkpoint improves the product, not just measures the cohort — adapted from Compound Engineering ("every unit of work leaves the system smarter"): (1) read the data against what the design assumed; (2) extract ≤3 learnings tagged Teaching / Product / Assessment; (3) encode them — log to Notion "🌱 Learnings & Compounding" in the AI Team Sprint Space, update the relevant master template, flag skill edits; (4) ship ONE visible improvement to the very next session and name it in the prep.
