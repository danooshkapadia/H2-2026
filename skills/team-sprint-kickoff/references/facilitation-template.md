# Facilitation Guide Template

Use this structure when building the kickoff call facilitation guide for any AI Team Sprint client.

---

## Header Block

```
# {COMPANY} TEAM SPRINT: KICKOFF CALL GUIDE
## {Day of week} {Date} · {Time} {Timezone} · {Duration} minutes
```

---

## Your One Job on This Call

Always state the primary objective in one sentence. For the sponsor kickoff, it is almost always:

> **Hand off from {Sponsor} to the team lead.** Everything else is secondary.

Then list the 4-6 concrete outcomes that define success:

1. A named team lead with a direct relationship to Danoosh
2. Discovery interviews calendared (or agreement on when they get calendared)
3. Tech setup call calendared (Danoosh + team lead + Brad)
4. Onboarding/orientation materials requested with a deadline
5. Sponsor's role clarified: sponsor, not project manager
6. Vacation/scheduling conflicts surfaced for the program window

---

## Before the Call Checklist (5 min)

- [ ] Calendar open on screen (scheduling live)
- [ ] Brad's availability confirmed for tech setup call
- [ ] This guide on second screen
- [ ] SOW open for reference (cohort size, dates, deliverables)
- [ ] Deep breath. You know this material.

---

## Read the Room (0:00 to 0:02)

Always include the three-scenario table:

| Who shows up | What it means | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| **Sponsor + team lead** | Best case. Run the full guide. | SCENARIO A |
| **Sponsor solo** | Common. Forgot or hasn't confirmed. | SCENARIO B |
| **No-show** | Possible. Client pattern. | SCENARIO C |

---

## SCENARIO A: Sponsor + Team Lead (Full Run)

### Block 1: Set the Frame ({duration} min)

**Open warm, then shift to agreements.**

Use this language pattern:

> "Before we jump in, I want to set up how this program actually runs, because it's different from a course or a training you sign up for and show up to. This is a working program. And the three of us each have a specific role that makes it work. So I want to get clear on those roles today, not as expectations, but as agreements. Things we're each choosing to commit to, eyes open."

**The Three Roles:**

For each role, state the agreements, then close with: "Does that feel right to you? Is there anything you'd want to add or change?"

**Executive Sponsor ({Sponsor name}):**
- Sets the tone from the top
- NOT running logistics, NOT chasing attendance, NOT the middleman
- Three touchpoints: kickoff, mid-program check-in, final readout
- Close: "Does that feel right to you?"

**Team Lead ({Name or TBD}):**
- Danoosh's operational partner for the duration
- Owns the Teams/Slack channel
- Posts challenges, drives completion, flags blockers
- Also a full participant, learning alongside the team
- Close: "How does that land? What questions do you have?"

**Program Lead (Danoosh):**
- Designs and delivers all sessions
- Builds the context file
- Sends recaps, recordings, challenges
- Delivers capability report
- What he won't do: chase people, manage attendance, troubleshoot IT beyond setup call

**Close this block:** "So those are the three roles. Are we all in agreement on those?"

### Block 2: The Pre-Sprint Plan ({duration} min)

Walk through what happens before Session 1 and calendar it live.

**Discovery interviews:**
- How many needed (per SOW)
- Who the sponsor recommends (get diverse sample: resistant, enthusiastic, operational)
- Agreement: email intros by {date}

**Tech setup call:**
- Danoosh + team lead + Brad
- Duration: 60 min
- Purpose: workspace config, permissions, roles, data controls, context file loading
- Calendar live on the call if possible
- Agreement: all participants have active licenses before this call

**Onboarding/orientation materials:**
- What to send: employee handbook, values, brand guidelines, strategic plan, anything a new hire gets
- Agreement: send by {date}

**Participant roster:**
- Final list: names, roles, email addresses
- Confirm cohort size against SOW range
- Agreement: confirm by {date}

### Block 3: Program Shape ({duration} min)

Brief overview of the sessions. Sponsor has seen this. Team lead hasn't.

Walk through each session at headline level. Don't go deep. This is orientation, not curriculum review.

Mention the between-session rhythm: recap → challenge posted → team works in-flow → next session opens.

**Lock the weekly session time** or get agreement on when team lead will poll and confirm.

### Block 4: Capability Assessment ({duration} min)

Position at "what it does" altitude:

> "Before we start, I'll send each participant a short assessment. It measures where they are with AI across a few key dimensions. That gives us a baseline. At the midpoint, I do a quick pulse check. At the end, the full assessment again. Then I give you a report that shows who shifted, where the team is strong, and where the gaps are."

If sponsor asks for more detail: "I'm finalizing the instrument this week. I'll share it with you before I send it to the team."

### Block 5: Confirm Agreements and Close ({duration} min)

**Read back every agreement.** This is the Chandler move.

Use a table format:

| Agreement | Owner | By When |
|---|---|---|
| {Agreement 1} | {Name} | {Date} |
| {Agreement 2} | {Name} | {Date} |
| ... | ... | ... |

Close: "{Team lead}, from here on out, you and I are going to be in direct contact. I'll send you a follow-up email today with everything we just agreed to. {Sponsor}, you're going to hear from me at the mid-program check-in unless something comes up."

---

## SCENARIO B: Sponsor Shows Up Solo

Adjust scope to 30-45 minutes.

**Adjusted goals:**
1. Get the team lead confirmed and named, TODAY
2. Get sponsor to send a warm intro email to the team lead DURING the call or immediately after
3. Calendar a separate 30-min call with the team lead this week
4. Cover roster, discovery names, onboarding materials request
5. Do NOT run the full program walkthrough. Save for the team lead call.

**Key questions:**
- "Who's your team lead?" If undecided, set a deadline: "I need a name by {date}."
- "Who's in the cohort?" Get names and roles.
- "Who should I talk to for discovery?" Get 2-3 names.

Close with: "I need {items} after this call. I'll set up a separate call with the team lead this week."

---

## SCENARIO C: No-Show

1. Wait 10 minutes
2. Send ONE text: "Hey {Sponsor}, we had {time} on the calendar for the Team Sprint kickoff. Want to reschedule for later this week?"
3. Do not chase beyond one text
4. Use the time to draft the follow-up email template
5. If they respond within the hour, offer a quick 15-min Scenario B

---

## Things to Watch For

Customize these for each client based on their psychology and patterns. Common ones:

| Watch For | Redirect |
|-----------|----------|
| Sponsor goes deep on their personal coaching topics | "Let's keep today focused on the Team Sprint. We'll pick that up in our next session." |
| Sponsor keeps selling you on why the team needs this | Let them talk 60 seconds, then redirect. They already bought. You need logistics. |
| Team lead seems nervous about the role | "Your job isn't to be the AI expert. Your job is to keep the rhythm going. I'll give you everything you need." |
| Sponsor asks about Phase 3 / command center / advanced AI | "That's where this is heading long-term. The Sprint builds the foundation. We'll talk about what's next in the Post-Sprint wrap." |
| HIPAA / governance questions | "The program assumes you have your own data handling policies in place. We can talk through a lightweight framework, but drafting that is on your side." |
| Cohort size drifts above SOW range | Flag to Danoosh silently. Don't correct the sponsor live. Handle in plus/delta. |

---

## Product Systemization Note

After every kickoff, capture:
- What worked in this guide vs. what to change
- Questions asked that the guide didn't prepare for
- Actual time per block vs. estimates
- Scope drift from SOW (if any)

This feeds Version {N+1} of the guide for the next client.
