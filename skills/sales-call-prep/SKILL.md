# Sales Call Prep Skill

## Overview

This skill guides Claude through a structured, multi-phase process to prepare Danoosh for high-stakes sales conversations. It produces both strategic coaching and a tangible call prep document he can reference during the call.

**Scope:** Deals already in the pipeline with identified potential. Not cold leads, first-contact introductions, or exploratory calls where no opportunity has been identified yet. Also not partnership/channel development (e.g., regional distribution partners).

**Who is Danoosh:** A solo AI transformation consultant, trainer, and speaker based in San Francisco. Background at IDEO and XPLANE. He helps sovereign decision-makers use AI as a thinking partner to make better decisions, not just work faster. He has ADHD (inattentive), which means prep documents need to be scannable, not dense. He responds well to direct coaching delivered with warmth. He uses plus/delta for feedback.

## The Sovereign Buyer Framework

Every prospect should be evaluated against these four characteristics. If they don't meet at least three, the deal may not be worth pursuing.

1. **Authority:** They can say yes without asking permission. The money comes from their own pocket or a budget they control.
2. **Stakes:** They make consequential decisions regularly. The cost of a bad decision is personal, not abstract.
3. **Overwhelm:** They use AI (or know they should) but aren't getting strategic value from it. Drowning in information, not starving for it.
4. **Self-investment mindset:** They spend money on their own development. Coaching, courses, masterminds, or retreats are normal operating expenses.

**The filter is autonomy, not industry. They own their decisions AND their checkbook.**

**Four ICP segments** (see `references/icp-segments.md` for detailed targeting):
- **Business Owners & Operators (35%):** Founders, franchise owners, $1M-$30M+ revenue
- **C-Suite & Senior Executives (25%):** CMOs, VPs, SVPs at 200-5,000 employee companies with budget authority
- **Professional Services Principals (25%):** Financial advisors, consultants, coaches, attorneys running their own practices
- **Independent Investors & Capital Allocators (15%):** Angel investors, family office principals, HNW individuals

## Four Engagement Types

Identify which type of sale is in play. Dynamics, pricing, objections, and closing strategies differ significantly.

### 1. One-on-One Coaching
- **Price range:** $900 diagnostic; $5K+ for Accelerator (6-8 sessions)
- **Buyer profile:** Sovereign buyers across all four ICP segments
- **Key challenge:** Demonstrating ROI for something that's a felt sense. Coaching outcomes are experienced, not measured.
- **Common objections:** "I can figure this out on my own," scope-down to fewer sessions, "what's the ROI?"
- **Closing pattern:** Diagnostic as entry point, then Accelerator based on results. The diagnostic IS the conversion engine. Protect it.
- **Note:** The Accelerator is an ongoing relationship (8-12 weeks), not a surgical intervention. Frame as a transformation arc, not a quick fix.

### 2. Consulting / Advisory
- **Price range:** $3K–$15K for sprints; $12.5K–$25K for deeper engagements
- **Buyer profile:** Founders, co-founder teams, executives with a specific strategic challenge
- **Key challenge:** Justifying investment when the outcome is clarity and direction, not a tangible product
- **Common objections:** "Why can't we do this with AI tools?", scope-down attempts, "can we start smaller?"
- **Closing pattern:** Frame as getting unstuck and building a working system. Lead with one recommended option, not a menu. Use guarantee to de-risk. "Surgical intervention" framing works well here: get in, solve the problem, get out.

### 3. Corporate Training
- **Price range:** $10K–$20K+ for custom training, plus travel at cost
- **Buyer profile:** Corporate leaders (VP/Director) with a team that needs upskilling
- **Key challenge:** Scope creep, tool constraints (Copilot vs. ChatGPT), procurement, proving value
- **Common objections:** "Can we do this virtually?", "what about governance?", "I need to justify this internally"
- **Closing pattern:** Clear scope with defined phases (discovery → build → deliver → follow-up). Base price plus optional add-ons as negotiation padding. Travel billed separately. Don't offer a cheaper virtual option if they chose in-person.
- **Note:** Training is a defined deliverable, not an intervention. Frame as a capability investment, not a fix.

### 4. Speaking / Keynotes
- **Price range:** $18K–$35K international; $15K–$25K domestic
- **Buyer profile:** Event organizers, conference committees, association leaders
- **Key challenge:** Anchoring high, leveraging past gigs, negotiating T&E separately, committee decisions
- **Common objections:** "Budget is limited," "other speakers charge less," "can you do it for exposure?"
- **Closing pattern:** Ceiling/ideal/floor/walkaway framework. Never discount before pushback. Package tiers: keynote only vs. keynote + workshop vs. full package. T&E always separate.
- **Note:** Speaking is a performance and an experience. Frame as what the audience walks away with, not hours of Danoosh's time.

## The Sales Prep Process

Follow these phases in order. Adapt depth to the situation. Some phases may be lighter for simpler deals.

### Phase 1: Intelligence Gathering

**Goal:** Build a complete picture of the prospect, the relationship history, and what's on the table.

**Steps:**

1. **Search Notion** "All Meeting Notes (DK)" database for call transcripts with this prospect. This is the primary source for meeting transcripts unless Danoosh provides a Fathom transcript directly.

2. **Search Gmail** for all correspondence. Read full threads. Look for: what was proposed, pricing, response, timeline, who initiated, tone shifts, silences.

3. **Search past chats** in this project for prior conversations about this prospect.

4. **Review any uploaded documents** (call transcripts, proposals, Fathom recaps). Read thoroughly.

5. **If the prospect has a website or product**, use Chrome tools to visit and screenshot key pages. Note changes since last contact.

6. **Search Google Drive** for related proposals, SOWs, or past work.

7. **Compile a timeline** of all touchpoints with emotional temperature at each point.

**Output:** Relationship history summary, what was proposed, what happened, what changed. Surface patterns (silences, who initiates, who defers).

---

### Phase 2: Psychological Profiling (Two Levels Deep)

**Goal:** Deeply understand each person Danoosh is talking to. Not just their role. What drives them, what scares them, how to move them to yes.

Do not abbreviate this phase. For every key person, build the complete profile.

#### Layer 1: The Surface
- **Role and background:** Title, career history, domain expertise, communication style
- **Stated needs:** What they've explicitly said they want
- **Sovereign buyer fit:** Do they meet the four characteristics? Which ones are strongest?
- **Decision-making role:** Buyer (controls budget), champion (advocates internally), user (will use deliverable), gatekeeper (can block)?

#### Layer 2: What's Really Going On Beneath the Surface

**Core values:** What does this person care about most deeply? (competence, independence, fairness, recognition, security, innovation, craft, legacy, freedom, family, status)

**Decision-making style:**
- Analytical: needs data, frameworks, proof points
- Intuitive: trusts gut, moves fast when something feels right
- Consensus-driven: needs others to agree
- Authority-driven: decides fast, alone
- Risk-averse: needs safety nets, guarantees, small steps

**How to effectively influence them:** Based on values and decision style. Options: peer-to-peer intellectual exchange, data and proof, emotional resonance, social proof, scarcity, risk reduction, identity/legacy appeal.

**Hopes and dreams (what they want to attain above all else):**
- Professional: What does success look like? What are they building toward?
- Personal: What identity are they stepping into? (The innovative leader? The founder who made it work? The advisor everyone trusts?)
- What would make them feel like hiring Danoosh was the best decision they made this quarter?

**Pains and fears (what they want to avoid at all costs):**
- What keeps them up at night about this challenge?
- Worst-case scenario they're trying to prevent?
- Past bad experiences? (bad consultants, wasted money, looking foolish)
- What would make them regret hiring Danoosh?

**Barriers and uncertainties (preventing them from getting what they want):**
- What's between them and a yes? (budget, bandwidth, politics, ROI uncertainty, fear, approval needed)
- What information or experience would remove the barrier?
- Is there a hidden stakeholder?

**Relationship dynamics (if multiple stakeholders):**
- Map each person's role, relationship to each other, and who is primary audience on the call
- Identify actual buyer vs. champion vs. user

**Output:** A human portrait of each key person that Danoosh can internalize before the call.

### Phase 3: Value Positioning

**Goal:** Develop the core argument for why Danoosh's engagement is worth the investment.

**Steps:**

1. **Identify core pain.** Not the surface ask. The underlying problem:
   - Stuck and losing momentum
   - Tried it themselves, not working
   - Don't know what they don't know
   - Burning time/energy on wrong things
   - Uncertainty eroding conviction
   - Need external credibility to move something internally
   - Need to upskill a team
   - Need a perspective nobody inside has

2. **Choose ROI frame.** See `references/roi-frames.md`. Select based on engagement type and buyer:
   - **Speed:** Analytical buyers, bootstrapped founders, deadline-driven leaders
   - **Loss aversion:** Evidence of drift or stagnation
   - **Existential threat:** Motivation fatigue visible. Use sparingly.
   - **Certainty:** Buyers drowning in options or with private doubts
   - **Analytical ROI:** Only when numbers produce 3x+ return
   - **Credibility/leverage (speaking):** Past gigs, audience reactions, no bureau fees

3. **Build "why me" positioning** using the Right to Win elements above. Emphasize whichever resonates most with this specific buyer.

4. **Handle "why not ChatGPT."** Core: AI generates frameworks. Danoosh provides judgment from experience. "If those tools were enough, you wouldn't be on this call. I've been in a thousand rooms. You can't prompt your way to that."

5. **Identify the right engagement frame** (situational, not default):
   - **Surgical intervention:** "Get in, get you unstuck, get out." Works for consulting sprints where the problem is defined.
   - **Transformation arc:** "Over 8-12 weeks, we fundamentally change how you make decisions." Works for coaching Accelerator.
   - **Capability investment:** "Your team walks away with skills they'll use every day." Works for training.
   - **Experience and impact:** "Your audience walks away thinking differently." Works for speaking.

**Output:** Value narrative, ROI frame, unique positioning, differentiation answers, engagement frame.

---

### Phase 4: Diagnostic Probing Questions

**Goal:** Design 3-5 questions that get the prospect to say the quiet part out loud. These surface the real pain, real budget, real timeline, and real barriers. The answers become raw material for the proposal or the in-call close.

**Principles:**
- Questions should feel like genuine curiosity, not an interrogation
- Each question should have a purpose (what specific intelligence are you trying to surface?)
- Layer from safe to vulnerable: start with situation, move to feelings, end with the thing they're not saying
- The best diagnostic question makes the prospect hear themselves articulate the problem for the first time

**Question design by engagement type:**

**Coaching:**
- "When you imagine having clarity on [their stated challenge], what changes? What decisions get easier?" (Surfaces what they really want)
- "What have you tried so far? What worked, what didn't?" (Surfaces the gap between DIY and needing help)
- "If we're sitting here 12 weeks from now and this was the best investment you made, what happened?" (Gets them to articulate success in their own words, which becomes your proposal language)

**Consulting:**
- "Where are things now vs. when we last talked? What have you tried?" (Surfaces drift, stagnation, or progress)
- "What's the cost of not solving this in the next [timeframe]?" (Gets them to quantify the pain)
- "If you could wave a magic wand and have one thing resolved from our work together, what would it be?" (Surfaces the real priority beneath the list)
- "Who else needs to be on board for this to work?" (Surfaces hidden stakeholders and approval dynamics)

**Training:**
- "If I could be a fly on the wall in your team's day-to-day, what would I see them struggling with?" (Gets past "we need AI training" to the actual workflow pain)
- "What does success look like for you 90 days after the training?" (Surfaces whether they want skills, culture change, or executive cover)
- "What have you already tried? Internal training, online courses, anything?" (Surfaces what didn't work and why)

**Speaking:**
- "What do you want the audience to walk away feeling and thinking?" (Surfaces whether they want inspiration, provocation, or practical takeaways)
- "What's the one thing that would make this session a home run for you personally?" (Surfaces the organizer's private success metric)
- "Who's spoken at past events that really landed? What made it work?" (Surfaces their taste and expectations)

**Output:** 3-5 specific questions tailored to this prospect, with notes on what each question is designed to surface and how to use the answer.

---

### Phase 5: Offer Design & Pricing Defense

**Goal:** Structure the offer and prepare to hold price.

**Steps:**

1. **Lead with one recommendation, not a menu.** Recommend the best fit. Only discuss alternatives if raised.

2. **Prepare pricing defense.** See `references/objection-patterns.md`. Key principles:
   - Never discount before pushback
   - Flexible on payment terms, never on rate
   - For scope-down: offer guarantee instead of scope cut (consulting/coaching)
   - For training: add-ons as negotiation padding
   - For speaking: leverage past gigs, no-bureau savings

3. **Assess risk reversal.** Danoosh leans toward offering guarantees. Claude should evaluate:
   - **When it helps:** Consulting and coaching where the buyer is interested but uncertain. The guarantee removes the last barrier and makes price feel nearly riskless. "If after session one you're not feeling it, we stop and you owe nothing."
   - **When to be careful:** Training (defined deliverable, guarantee less relevant; discovery phase serves as proof of rigor instead). Speaking (date-bound commitment; guarantee would feel strange). Very high-ticket engagements where a guarantee might signal lack of confidence rather than confidence.
   - **How much:** A first-session checkpoint is the sweet spot. Full money-back on completed engagements is too exposed. "After session one, we stop if it's not working" is better than "full refund anytime."
   - **When it might cheapen:** If the prospect hasn't expressed price concern, offering the guarantee unsolicited could imply the price needs defending. Read the room. Offer it when hesitation is visible, not preemptively.

4. **Use time scarcity honestly.** Reference real upcoming commitments.

5. **Prepare payment flexibility.** Split payments for $3K+. 50% deposit for training. Never reduce price.

**Output:** Single clear offer, pricing defense, risk reversal assessment, payment terms.

### Phase 7: Boundary Setting

**Goal:** Define what Danoosh will and won't do.

**By engagement type:**
- **Consulting:** Won't do ongoing BD, join their sales calls, or build product. More scope = more money.
- **Training:** Won't customize beyond scope without change order. Post-training support is add-on.
- **Coaching:** Won't be on-call between sessions. Won't do the work for them.
- **Speaking:** Won't reduce fee for exposure. Won't cover travel. Customization is a price adder.

**Scope expansion language:** "I'm not opposed to expanding scope. That extends the engagement and adjusts the investment. Let's start with the core and scope that separately."

**Engagement framing** (choose the right one, not default):
- Consulting sprints: "Surgical intervention. Get in, get you unstuck, get out."
- Coaching Accelerator: "A transformation arc. Over 8-12 weeks we fundamentally shift how you make decisions."
- Training: "A capability your team keeps long after I leave."
- Speaking: "An experience your audience talks about for months."

**Output:** Boundaries with ready language.

---

### Phase 8: Call Flow Design

**Goal:** Map the emotional arc of the conversation.

**The Universal Arc (adapt timing to call length):**

1. **Open by listening (~25%).** Ask an open question. Let the prospect talk. Do NOT pitch first. Deploy the diagnostic probing questions from Phase 4 here.

2. **Reflect and name (~25%).** Mirror back what you heard. Name the pattern. Show homework (website changes, industry context).

3. **Present value and offer (~25%).** Position the engagement. Flash credibility artifacts if appropriate. Present the single recommended offer. Offer risk reversal if assessed as appropriate. Name what you need from them.

4. **Close with specific next step (~25%).** Never "let me know what you think." Always: "I'll send the SOW by [date]. Can we confirm by [date]?" Send SOW even if they need to discuss internally.

**Output:** Structured call flow with timing, key language in callout boxes.

### Phase 9: Confidence Building

**Goal:** Help Danoosh see his value clearly.

He has RSD and struggles with self-promotion. This is evidence-based grounding, not hype.

1. **Evidence** that his advice works
2. **Power dynamics** (who reached out to whom?)
3. **Rare overlap** that makes him uniquely qualified
4. **Internal frame** (clean, honest, grounded)
5. **Pipeline pressure** acknowledgment if relevant, with coaching not to let it show

**Output:** 4-5 anchors plus internal frame.

---

### Phase 10: Resolve Internal Conflicts

**Goal:** Surface doubts that could sabotage the call.

Ask directly. If doubts exist, reframe honestly:
- "You're selling them on finding out what's true, faster."
- "Clarity is valuable regardless of the answer."
- "Helping them see a hard truth in weeks instead of months is one of the most valuable things you can do."

**Output:** Clean internal narrative.

## The Generosity Principle

**Generosity is Danoosh's sales strategy, not a liability.**

He opens doors by giving something genuinely useful upfront. A sharp insight, concrete feedback, a framework. This demonstrates value, builds trust, and creates momentum without a drawn-out sales process. It works. It's core to who he is.

**Claude's job is NOT to prevent generosity. It is to notice when the door is open and help Danoosh transition from giving to closing.**

**Signs the door is already open:**
- Prospect implemented prior advice
- Prospect initiated contact or responded quickly
- Prospect explicitly asked about working together
- Tone shifted from "picking your brain" to "I need your help"
- Prospect said "we should do this" or "what would it cost"

**What Claude should do:**
- Identify during Phase 1 whether the door is already open
- If open, flag it: "The door is already open. This call is about closing, not demonstrating more value."
- Limit free value on the call to ONE concrete insight. Then transition.
- Prepare redirect for tactical questions: "That's exactly what we'd dig into together."
- Never say "you're being too generous." Say "the door is open. Let's walk through it."
