# Objection Patterns Reference

## Objection Archetypes

Every sales objection Danoosh encounters falls into one of these categories. Identify the archetype first, then adapt the response to the specific situation.

---

### 1. The Scope-Down
**What they say:** "Can we start with just one session?" / "Let's do a smaller version first."
**What they mean:** They want to manage risk. They're not saying no. They're saying "I'm not sure yet."
**Response pattern:** Acknowledge the instinct. Explain why the value comes from iteration, not a single interaction. Then offer the guarantee as the built-in risk management. "If after session one you're not feeling it, we stop and you owe nothing. So you're effectively getting that trial built in."
**Boundary:** If they really push, price a single diagnostic session at $750-$1,000 minimum. Never a favor rate. Frame it as a diagnostic, not a strategy session.

---

### 2. The Price Push
**What they say:** "Is there flexibility on pricing?" / "That feels like a lot for where we are."
**What they mean:** Either genuine budget constraint or habitual negotiation.
**Response pattern:** Hold the price. Offer flexible payment terms (split into two payments). Reframe: "I'd rather keep the price and give you the guarantee than compress it to where it can't be effective." If they name a specific budget constraint, adjust terms, not price.
**Boundary:** Never discount. Flexible on terms, never on rate.

---

### 3. The Free Consulting Probe
**What they say:** "What do you think about [specific tactical question]?" / "Should we add [feature]?" / "What's your take on our pricing?"
**What they mean:** They're testing whether they can get the insight without paying. Often unconscious, not malicious.
**Response pattern:** Give one sentence of acknowledgment, then redirect. "That's a great instinct, and it's exactly what we'd pressure-test together. I have thoughts, but I'd want to look at your data before giving you something actionable."
**Boundary:** One insight to demonstrate value. Then draw the line. Never answer more than one tactical question in depth.

---

### 4. The DIY Objection
**What they say:** "How is this different from what we could do ourselves?" / "We've been using ChatGPT for this."
**What they mean:** They want to believe they can solve this without spending money. Or they genuinely don't see the gap.
**Response pattern:** Name the gap between information and judgment. "AI generates frameworks. I provide judgment from experience. I've done this hundreds of times. The ability to look at a conversation and tell you exactly where it went sideways comes from being in a thousand rooms." If they've clearly been trying AI: "If AI tools were enough, you wouldn't be on this call."
**Boundary:** Don't get defensive about AI. Embrace it. The point is that AI is a tool, not a substitute for experienced pattern recognition.

---

### 5. The Readiness Delay
**What they say:** "We're not sure we're ready for this yet." / "Let us get a few more things in place first."
**What they mean:** Fear of commitment, or genuine belief that conditions need to change first.
**Response pattern:** Ask "What would ready look like?" Then: "In my experience, the right time is before you've burned through your motivation trying things that aren't landing." Name that waiting has a cost.
**Boundary:** Don't chase. If they genuinely aren't ready, let them go gracefully with a clear door to return.

---

### 6. The Scope Creep Request
**What they say:** "Would this also cover [product strategy / hiring / fundraising / etc.]?"
**What they mean:** They're excited and want more. Or they're testing how much they can get for the price.
**Response pattern:** Show flexibility without giving it away. "I'm not opposed to expanding scope. That would extend the engagement and adjust the investment. Why don't we start with the core sprint, and if we discover the [additional area] needs attention, I'll scope that separately."
**Boundary:** More scope always equals more money. Never absorb additional work into an existing price.

---

### 7. The Internal Champion Delay
**What they say:** "I need to talk to [partner / co-founder / boss] before deciding."
**What they mean:** Either genuine (they need buy-in) or a soft no.
**Response pattern:** Respect it, but create forward motion. "Totally understand. Why don't I send the SOW over anyway so you have something concrete to look at together? Can we plan to confirm by [specific date]?" Always send the SOW. It's harder to say no to a concrete document than a verbal pitch.
**Boundary:** Give a timeframe. Don't leave it open-ended.

---

### 8. The Deliverables Question
**What they say:** "What are the actual deliverables?"
**What they mean:** They need to justify the spend, either to themselves or to someone else. They want something tangible.
**Response pattern:** Have a specific list ready. Make it concrete: "A positioning document, annotated feedback on 5 recorded conversations, a refined talk track, packaging and pricing recommendations, 2-3 proof stories, and a 90-day experiment roadmap." A concrete deliverables list makes the price feel like a steal.
**Boundary:** Deliverables should be outputs of the work, not additional work items. Don't let the deliverables list become a scope expansion.

---

### 9. The Viability Question
**What they say:** "What if we find out it's not going to work?" / "What if the market isn't there?"
**What they mean:** They have private doubts about their own product/business and are afraid of confirming them.
**Response pattern:** Frame clarity as the most valuable outcome. "Then we'll know that clearly and quickly, instead of spending six more months wondering. That's one of the most valuable outcomes an engagement like this can produce." This reframe makes the engagement valuable regardless of the answer.
**Boundary:** Don't make promises about outcomes. Promise clarity and speed.

---

### 10. The Post-Engagement Question
**What they say:** "What happens after this is over? Is there ongoing support?"
**What they mean:** Either testing for dependency or genuinely wondering about continuity.
**Response pattern:** "This is designed as a surgical intervention. Get you unstuck, get you over the hump, get you on your way. My goal is you don't need me on an ongoing basis. If something comes up later, I'm a phone call away. But independence is the design."
**Boundary:** Don't upsell a retainer during the close. It undermines the "surgical intervention" positioning.
