# Closing Techniques Reference

## Principles

Danoosh sells as a trusted advisor, not a salesperson. Aggressive closing techniques (hard closes, artificial urgency, manipulation) are off-brand and will backfire. The techniques below are all rooted in clarity, warmth, and forward motion.

---

## The Specific Next Step Close

**The single most important closing technique.** Never end a call with "let me know what you think." Always propose a concrete next step with a date.

**Pattern:** "If this feels right, I'll send a short SOW by [day]. Can we confirm by [day]?"

**Why it works:** It assumes the yes without being presumptuous. It creates momentum. It gives the prospect something to react to (a document) rather than something to decide in the abstract.

---

## The Guarantee Close

**When to use:** When the prospect is interested but hesitant about risk.

**Pattern:** "If after our first session you don't feel like we're headed somewhere valuable, we stop and you owe nothing. I've never had to do that, but the risk should be on me to deliver, not on you to hope it works out."

**Key elements:**
- Frame as a professional commitment, not a product return policy
- "I've never had to do that" is a quiet confidence signal
- Structure as a stopping-point guarantee (after session 1), not an after-the-fact guarantee
- This makes the real decision not "$5K vs. $0" but "$5K vs. the cost of one session"

---

## The Time Window Close

**When to use:** When you have genuine upcoming commitments that constrain availability.

**Pattern:** "I have availability to start the week of [date]. I have a project starting [later date] that takes most of my bandwidth. If we start now, I can give you my full attention."

**Key elements:**
- Must be honest. Never fabricate scarcity.
- Creates a natural decision window without pressure
- Especially effective when combined with the argument that doing the work in phases (cheaper option now, more later) loses the feedback loop

---

## The SOW-As-Anchor Close

**When to use:** When the prospect says they need to discuss internally before deciding.

**Pattern:** "Totally understand. Why don't I send the SOW over anyway so you have something concrete to look at together? Can we plan to confirm by [specific date]?"

**Why it works:** A written document is harder to ignore than a verbal pitch. It creates a concrete artifact that the champion can use internally. It also establishes the price as a fact rather than an opening bid.

---

## The Reframe Close

**When to use:** When the prospect is comparing your engagement to a cheaper alternative (smaller scope, DIY, etc.).

**Pattern:** "The [cheaper option] gives you a plan. The [recommended option] gives you a skill / system / capability. A plan without execution support is what you've already been trying."

**Key elements:**
- Position the cheaper alternative as the riskier choice
- The $2K difference (or whatever the delta) isn't "more of the same." It's "the part that actually matters."
- Never frame as "you can't afford to go cheap." Frame as "the cheaper option won't solve your problem."

---

## The Parallel-Not-Sequential Close

**When to use:** When the prospect wants to start with a smaller engagement and add on later.

**Pattern:** "The strategy work and the [execution/sales/implementation] work aren't two separate steps. They run in parallel. I build the strategy and we immediately test it against reality. If we separate them, the strategy sits on a shelf and you're back to guessing."

**Why it works:** It eliminates the "start small and upgrade" path by showing that the parts are interdependent, not modular.

---

## The Payment Terms Pivot

**When to use:** When price is the stated objection but the prospect clearly wants to proceed.

**Pattern:** "If cash flow is the concern, we can split it: [half] to start, [half] at the midpoint. But I don't want to cut the [key element] because that's where the value lives."

**Key elements:**
- Flexible on terms, never on price
- Always name the thing you won't cut and why
- This signals that the price is firm while showing empathy for their financial reality

---

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **"Let me know what you think"** — Opens the door to infinite delay. Always close with a specific step.
- **"Feel free to reach out anytime"** — Reopens free advisory channels. Close the loop.
- **"I could probably do it for less"** — Premature discounting. Never offer a discount before they ask for one.
- **"We could start with something small"** — The foot-in-the-door instinct. Challenge it. The right engagement is usually bigger than Danoosh's first instinct.
- **"I'm just happy to help"** — Signals that the work isn't worth paying for. Help is free. Expertise costs money.
