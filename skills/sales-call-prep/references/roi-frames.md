# ROI Frames Reference

## When to Use Which Frame

The analytical ROI frame (subscriber math, revenue projections) is often the weakest frame for early-stage or bootstrapped prospects. Use it only when the numbers produce a compelling 3x+ return. Otherwise, choose from the frames below.

---

## Speed Frame
**Best for:** Analytical buyers, bootstrapped founders, anyone who values efficiency.

**Core argument:** The engagement compresses months of experimentation into weeks. The alternative isn't "save $5K." The alternative is "spend 6 more months guessing."

**Key language:**
- "The $X isn't buying you a plan. It's buying you speed."
- "For a bootstrapped startup, speed is the most valuable thing there is."
- "I compress what might take six to nine months of experimentation into four weeks."

---

## Loss Aversion Frame
**Best for:** Prospects who've been stuck, who've tried things that didn't work, where there's visible drift.

**Core argument:** The cost of continued drift (time, energy, motivation) exceeds the engagement cost. Name what they've already lost without being cruel about it.

**Key language:**
- "Every month without clarity is a month of runway, energy, and motivation you're burning."
- "The changes you've made are surface-level execution. The deeper strategic questions are still open."
- "If you keep iterating on the website instead of the sales conversation, another three months goes by and you're in the same spot with less energy."

**Warning:** Don't frame this as "you wasted time." Frame it as "the hard part is still ahead and it gets harder with delay."

---

## Existential Threat Frame
**Best for:** Founders/leaders where motivation fatigue is visible. Use sparingly and briefly.

**Core argument:** The real danger isn't failure. It's the slow erosion of belief that comes from prolonged uncertainty.

**Key language:**
- "The biggest risk isn't running out of money. It's running out of belief."
- "Every month without traction erodes conviction. Not because the product is wrong, but because the uncertainty is exhausting."
- "I've seen founders slowly lose momentum, not because they gave up, but because they never got the clarity they needed to push through the hard part."

---

## Certainty Frame
**Best for:** Buyers who are overwhelmed by options, who keep asking "what should we do first?"

**Core argument:** You're not selling deliverables. You're selling resolution of ambiguity.

**Key language:**
- "You're not buying a plan. You're buying resolution of uncertainty."
- "My job is to help you find out what's true, faster."
- "The engagement is valuable whether the answer is 'push forward' or 'pivot.' Either way, you stop guessing."

---

## Combining Frames

The most effective approach usually layers 2-3 frames. A common sequence:

1. Open with **loss aversion** (name the drift)
2. Escalate briefly with **existential threat** (name what drift leads to)
3. Close with **speed** (position the engagement as the solution)

The **certainty frame** is an overlay that works with any of the above, especially when the prospect has private doubts about their own product or strategy.
