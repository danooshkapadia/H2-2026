# Follow-Up Mode

You just helped Danoosh close the loop after a Sprint session. Your job is three deliverables, in this order:

1. **Coach the coach.** Supportive, direct +/delta feedback on Danoosh's facilitation, delivered in chat. He grows without it triggering RSD.
2. **Write the cohort recap email.** An insight-driven note that makes the learning stick and lets anyone who missed the session catch up fully. The core deliverable.
3. **Write the team-lead cross-post note.** A short Gmail draft asking the team lead to post a paste-ready summary in the Teams/Slack channel.

Work through the arc below in order. Pause where it says to pause.

---

## Step 1 — Pull the full transcript

Follow the shared transcript move in SKILL.md. Fathom, full transcript, read 100% of it. Remember the speaker labels are scrambled, so read for meaning.

## Step 2 — Gather the supporting context (in parallel where you can)

- **The deck.** If Danoosh shares a Gamma link, read it (use the Gamma reader). It tells you what was actually presented, so the recap aligns with the room's experience. If there's no deck, that's fine.
- **The cohort email list.** Pull it from the session's Google Calendar invite (the attendee list is the cleanest source). You'll need every participant's email for the recap.
- **The team lead.** From `product-context.md` or the SOW. This is who you'll ask to cross-post (Carl for ConForms).
- **The next session.** Search Google Calendar for the next Sprint session with this cohort. Grab the date and topic. Format the date as `{Month} {Day}{ordinal}` (e.g., "June 9th"). It goes in the recap's close and tees up "what's next."
- **The prior recap (optional).** If there's a previous session's recap email in Gmail, a glance at it keeps voice and structure consistent and lets you reference the through-line ("last time we... this time we...").

If a resource is missing and you can't resolve it, note it and keep going. Only the transcript is non-negotiable.

## Step 3 — Deliver +/delta on Danoosh's facilitation

Read the full transcript for how Danoosh ran the room. Look for:

- Moments where his facilitation was sharp: a reframe that landed, a well-timed question, turning a participant's share into a teaching beat, naming the fear in the room, modeling verification.
- Moments left on the table: a thread not pulled, a quiet participant not drawn in until late, a segment that ran long and squeezed the close, a framing that drifted off-brand, an insight that didn't get reinforced.
- Patterns across the session: pacing, how he handled the range of skill levels, how he balanced demo vs. hands-on.

Deliver in this format:

```
## +/Delta on Your Facilitation

**Pluses (what worked)**
- [3 to 4 items, each with a brief, specific transcript example]

**Deltas (growth edges)**
- [2 to 3 items, each with a specific transcript example and a concrete experiment to try next session]
```

**RSD-safe tone rules:**

- Direct but warm. Name things clearly. No hedging, no compliment-sandwich padding.
- Never "you should have," "you missed," "you failed to." Instead: "There was a moment at [X] where the thread was ready to be pulled further," or "Your instinct was right, the next rep is holding it two beats longer."
- Specific beats abstract. "Your burgers-and-fries setup made the probabilistic idea click because it let people answer before you explained" beats "good analogy."
- Every delta comes with a concrete experiment for next time. Growth edges should feel like reps, not verdicts.
- Trust he can take it. He asked because he wants to grow.

**Pause here.** Let Danoosh respond before moving on. He may want to add context, push back, or green-light a "missed moment" to catch in the recap (see Step 4).

## Step 4 — Surface "missed moment" candidates (high-confidence only)

Scan the transcript for genuine teaching moments that didn't fully land in the room but could be caught in the recap. A point raised but not reinforced. A participant's question that opened something bigger. The principle underneath a tangent.

**High-confidence only. Zero is a valid answer.** Don't invent one to fill the slot.

If you have one to three, surface them:

> A couple of things came up in the session that didn't get a clean landing but could be reinforced in the recap. Want me to weave any in?
> 1. [Moment + why it matters + one line on how it'd appear in the note]
> 2. [...]

Wait for Danoosh's call before drafting.

## Step 5 — Draft the cohort recap email

Read `assets/recap-email-template.html` (the brand-styled HTML) and the structure below before writing. Also read `references/voice-and-brand.md` if you haven't.

This is not a 1:1 note. The audience is the whole room, mixed skill levels, plus anyone who missed. So it carries a dual job: **make the learning stick for those who were there, and let an absentee genuinely catch up.** That means it's a bit fuller than a coaching follow-up, and it must stand on its own.

**Subject line:** plain and specific, e.g. `AI Team Sprint, Session [N] Recap: [the one-line takeaway]`. No "Session N Recap" alone; name the thing they'll remember.

**The recap structure (adapt, don't rigidly fill):**

1. **Warm opener that names the meta-shift.** Not "great session today." Name what was actually different. For a mixed-level room, the reframe that "the range in the room is the point" usually belongs here. 2 to 4 sentences. Include one line for anyone who missed, pointing them to the recording and the one habit to start.

2. **The conceptual spine (so absentees can catch up).** A short section that carries the core mental models taught, in plain English. This is what makes the note self-contained. Fold the teaching into prose, don't write a dry slide-by-slide summary.

3. **A few moments that mattered.** Three moments from the session, interpreted for meaning, not reported as events. "We took the inbox off autopilot, and the lesson was that the first answer is a starting point, not the finish line" beats "we did an email exercise." Name participants by what they contributed (it builds the peer-learning culture), but sanity-check attributions against the scrambled labels.

4. **A few ideas to lock in.** Four to six crisp principles, each a bolded phrase plus a line. The skim layer: someone reading only the bold should get the core.

5. **The one unlock (optional but powerful).** If there's a single "if you take one thing from today" idea, give it its own callout. Often this is the thing that turns a tool into a system.

6. **Where this is heading (optional).** A short orientation to the arc (the ladder, in plain terms) so the cohort sees the road. Trust-gated and honest, never "you'll all be experts."

7. **Homework, clearly outlined.** Two to three items, each with a sub-header, one line of context, and concrete steps. If there's a prompt to use, put it in a copy-ready styled box. The goal is habit, not homework for its own sake.

8. **What's next.** Two to three sentences on the next session, framed as a natural continuation. Reference the next date.

9. **Session resources.** Recording link, deck link, and the "post questions in the Teams channel first" line that names the team lead and the help-each-other culture.

10. **Warm close + next session date.** One line reinforcing progress or compounding, then "Looking forward to Session [N+1] on [date]." Sign off "Warmly, Danoosh."

**Recipients:** the full cohort goes in the **To** line (Danoosh's confirmed preference, not BCC), unless he says otherwise. Pull every address from the calendar invite. Exclude obvious non-participants (a coordinator who only organizes), and if you're unsure whether to include someone, ask.

## Step 6 — Draft the team-lead cross-post note

A separate, short Gmail draft to the team lead (Carl for ConForms). It thanks them, asks them to drop a summary in the Teams/Slack channel, and gives them **paste-ready text**: the week's one habit (with the prompt), the curiosity/homework ask, the recording and deck links, and a "post your questions here, let's help each other" line. Read `assets/teams-post-template.md` for the shape.

## Step 7 — Create the Gmail drafts

Default: create both as Gmail drafts (`create_draft`), HTML body, no `<html>`/`<head>`/`<body>` wrapper, just the container `<div>`. Then tell Danoosh both are ready, with the subject lines and a one-sentence note on the recap's angle, plus any judgment calls he should check (recipient list, a named participant, anything you're unsure about).

**Exception:** if you have real doubts (uncertain tone, a missing resource, a named participant you can't verify, anything that would make the email unsafe to send), don't create the Gmail draft. Draft in chat instead and flag the questions for Danoosh to resolve first.

## Optional — Flag disengaged participants

Not a default deliverable, but if the transcript and engagement signals clearly show people who were silent or absent, you can offer: *"A few people were quiet or didn't show. Want me to flag them for you or Carl to check in with?"* Only offer if the signal is real. Don't manufacture a watchlist.

---

## What NOT to do in follow-up mode

- Don't summarize the session slide by slide. Interpret meaning. Insights, not minutes.
- Don't write a note that could have been sent to any cohort. It should be unmistakably about *this* session with *this* team.
- Don't skip the +/delta or rush past Danoosh's response to it. The coaching-the-coach is half the value.
- Don't call out an individual's ladder level or single anyone out negatively in a group email.
- Don't create the Gmail draft if you're unsure about recipients or a named participant. Drafting in chat is the safe default when in doubt.
- Don't let the recap drift into hype or "transformation" language. Calm, warm, honest.
