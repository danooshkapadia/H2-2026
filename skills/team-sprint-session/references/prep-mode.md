# Prep Mode

Danoosh has a Sprint session coming up and wants to walk in ready. The curriculum topic is mostly fixed (see `product-context.md`), so your job is not to invent a session from scratch. It's to **tune the planned session to where this specific cohort actually is** and hand Danoosh everything he needs to run it well.

Four deliverables, produced after a context check he signs off on:

1. **Cohort context summary** (in chat, for his confirmation)
2. **Facilitator cheat sheet** (his second-screen reference during the session)
3. **Gamma deck bullets** (on-screen slide content, ready to paste)
4. **Tailored exercises** (hands-on work built from the cohort's real tasks)

**Before you draft any of these, internalize `references/voice-and-brand.md`.** One rule trips up prep artifacts in particular: **do not use em dashes (—).** Use commas, periods, or colons instead. This matters everywhere, but it matters most in the Gamma deck bullets, which the whole cohort sees on screen, and a stray em dash instantly reads as not-Danoosh's-voice. When you finish each artifact, scan it for "—" and replace every one. Treat zero em dashes as the bar.

---

## Step 1 — Establish where the cohort sits (the sourcing ladder)

You can't tune a session without knowing where the team is. Source this read in layers, in this order, and stop as soon as you have a confident picture:

1. **The latest rubric/assessment file** in the client's folder (e.g., `ConForms_PreSprint_Survey_Rubric_Coding.md` or a coded assessment sheet). If it exists, it's the most accurate read: ladder distribution, individual placements, who to watch. Use it.
2. **The full Fathom transcript of the previous session.** If you're prepping Session N, pull Session N-1's complete transcript (the shared move in SKILL.md, full transcript not the summary). It tells you what actually landed, who engaged, who was quiet, what confused people, and what they're ready for next. For Session 1, there's no prior session, so lean on the pre-Sprint survey/rubric file and discovery notes.
3. **When in doubt, pause and ask Danoosh.** If the file is missing and the transcript is thin or unavailable, do not invent where the cohort sits. Ask him directly. A guessed ladder read produces a mistuned session.

Also gather, where available: the SOW (confirms this session's intended topic and any client-specific thread), discovery interview notes (real workflows to build exercises from), and the Teams/Slack channel activity if accessible (between-session engagement signals).

## Step 2 — Present the cohort context summary, then pause

Before building anything, give Danoosh a tight read so he can confirm or correct. Structure:

```
## Context Summary, [Client] Session [N]

**Session date:** [Day, Date, Time]
**Planned topic (from curriculum/SOW):** [e.g., Session 2, Getting Great Outputs]
**Where the cohort sits:** [Ladder read. Distribution if you have the file, estimate-flagged if inferred from the transcript.]
**What landed last session:** [1 to 2 sentences from the transcript]
**What didn't, or confused people:** [Anything that fell flat or raised questions]
**Engagement signals:** [Homework/channel activity, who's pulling ahead, who's quiet]
**People to watch:** [Specific names: who needs support, who can peer-teach, any wild cards]
**Client-specific thread:** [Anything tuned to this client, e.g. cross-cultural comms for ConForms]
**Gaps I couldn't fill:** [Be honest about what's missing]
```

If there's tension between the planned topic and where the cohort actually is (e.g., the curriculum says "thinking partner" but half the room is still shaky on drafting), name it explicitly and ask Danoosh how he wants to handle it. Don't resolve it yourself. He decides how to flex the session.

**Pause here.** Let him confirm the read and the direction before you build the artifacts.

## Step 3 — Build the facilitator cheat sheet

Read `assets/facilitator-cheatsheet-reference.md` for the structure. This is the document Danoosh has open on his second screen during the live session. It must be glanceable in two seconds at any point, not a script.

It covers: the cohort at a glance (ladder distribution, the headline read), the session flow with timing blocks, the key talking points and framings, people to watch (who to draw in, who can peer-teach, who to manage), phrases to use, and things not to say (brand voice guardrails). Tune every part to this cohort, using what you learned from the transcript and assessment data.

## Step 4 — Build the Gamma deck bullets

These are the on-screen slide contents Danoosh pastes into Gamma to generate the deck. Not speaker notes, not a script: the words the cohort sees.

Before building from scratch, check Danoosh's Notion AI Libraries (2025 and 2026) for existing deck content on this topic. If something reusable exists, tell him and offer to adapt it rather than rebuild.

Follow the brand voice in `references/voice-and-brand.md`. Keep slides clean and sparse (Swiss/Scandinavian aesthetic, lots of white space). Anchor concepts visually, then move to hands-on. Tie examples to the cohort's real world wherever you can. These slides are cohort-facing, so the no-em-dash rule is non-negotiable here: scan the finished bullets and replace every "—" with a comma, period, or colon.

## Step 5 — Build the tailored exercises

The exercises are where behavior change happens, so they must use the cohort's **actual work**, not generic examples. Pull real material from discovery notes and prior transcripts: ConForms' RMA returns, a messy supplier email thread, their SAP screens, Amada's VA authorizations and scheduling. Match the exercise to the AI tool the cohort uses (Copilot vs. ChatGPT have different menus and feature names, see `product-context.md`).

For each exercise, give Danoosh: the setup (what to tell the room), the exact prompt or steps participants run, the debrief question that turns the exercise into a lesson, and a note on what "good" looks like vs. where people will get stuck. Build a primary exercise plus a stretch option for the advanced participants so the range in the room stays engaged.

## Step 6 — Deliver and offer the deck

Save the cheat sheet (and exercises) as files, and present them. Put the Gamma bullets where Danoosh can grab them easily. Offer next steps: generate the actual Gamma deck, draft a pre-session nudge to the cohort ("come with X"), or adjust anything in the plan.

---

## What NOT to do in prep mode

- Don't invent where the cohort sits. Source it (file, then transcript), and if you can't, ask.
- Don't reinvent the curriculum. Tune the planned session; don't replace it without Danoosh's say-so.
- Don't build generic exercises. If it could be used at any company, it's not tuned enough. Use their real work.
- Don't overload the session. One core concept, one main build, plus a stretch for the advanced folks. No concept-stuffing.
- Don't build a deck from scratch before checking the AI Libraries for reusable content.
- Don't resolve a topic-vs-reality tension yourself. Surface it as a choice for Danoosh.
