# Voice and Brand

Read before drafting anything participant-facing (recap emails, cheat sheets, slide content, Teams posts). These rules come from Danoosh's brand and from the Sprint's design principles. They are why his work feels like his.

## Voice

- **Warm, conversational, human-centered.** Editor voice, not marketer voice. Think and write like an editor: clean, calm, grounded, quietly confident.
- **Plain English for every AI concept.** The test: a shop-floor supervisor and a VP of Engineering should both read it as easily. Explain like you're talking to a smart friend, never down to anyone.
- **Insight over recap.** Interpret what something meant; don't report what happened. "You moved from X to Y" beats "we covered X."
- **Trusted guide, on the journey with them.** Danoosh is in it alongside the team, not selling to them. He names his own struggle with the pace of AI. That honesty is what earns trust.
- **No hype, no false binaries, no tech-bro framing.** Avoid "transformation," "game-changing," "10x," "best practices," "AI literacy," "unlock your potential," "revolutionize." Nuance over contrast. He responds poorly to salesy or over-certain language, so his cohorts will too.
- **Sentences short enough to read in one breath.** Especially in participant-facing copy.
- **Limit em dashes.** Use commas, periods, or colons. This is a hard preference of Danoosh's. A stray em dash reads as not-his-voice.
- **Bold carries the skim layer.** In emails, someone reading only the bolded phrases should still get the core. Use bold sparingly and deliberately.

## Brand styling (for HTML emails and any designed artifact)

Danoosh's brand, approximated for email-client safety:

- **Headers / titles:** Arial or Helvetica, bold, black (#000000). (Helvetica Bold black is the real brand font.)
- **Sub-headers:** khaki / tan (#c5aa88), uppercase, letter-spaced. (Stand-in for the brand sub-header treatment.)
- **Body:** Georgia (a Century stand-in), ~16px, line-height 1.6, near-black (#1a1a1a).
- **Backgrounds / callout panels:** soft neutrals, #ece3d7 or #eaeaea for highlighted boxes (prompts, the "one unlock").
- **Red accent:** #cc0000, reserved for one or two uses max across an entire email. Emphasis, not decoration.
- **Layout:** asymmetrical Swiss grid, Scandinavian aesthetic. Clean, modern, sophisticated, lots of white space. Sparse beats busy.

The brand-styled recap email template in `assets/recap-email-template.html` already encodes these. Use it rather than reinventing the styling.

## Sponsor-facing tone (if a session deliverable ever touches the sponsor)

This skill is mostly cohort-facing, but if anything reaches the sponsor (Dave, Joe, Cindy):

- Honest, calm, grounded. Emotion first (the felt sense that this is working), data second (the numbers behind it).
- No marketing language. A progress report, never a sales document.
- Honest about gaps. A 100% glowing report reads as a pitch and erodes trust.

## A quick gut-check before sending anything

- Could this have been written for any cohort? If yes, it's too generic. Make it unmistakably about this team and this session.
- Did I use an em dash I could replace with a comma or period?
- Is there any word that sounds like a consultant or a vendor? Cut it.
- Would the most skeptical, least technical person in the room understand every sentence?
