# AI Team Sprint, Product Context

Read this once at the start of any session work. It holds what's true for every Sprint, regardless of client or session. Treat the curriculum, the ladder, and the voice rules as stable. Treat the client roster as a snapshot that may be out of date, verify against live sources when it matters.

---

## What the Sprint is

A six-week AI enablement program for one corporate team (up to ~15 by design, sometimes more). The format: a two-hour kickoff, then five 75-minute sessions at a 7 to 10 day cadence, plus a private Teams or Slack channel where the team lead posts applied challenges between sessions. Sessions are recorded and sent with recap emails.

The core thesis, which should shape everything you produce: **behavior change beats skill transfer.** Traditional AI training is a one or two-day intensive that delivers content and disappears; people retain 20 to 30% and nothing changes on Monday. The Sprint replaces that with a weekly drumbeat, real work between sessions, and a shared channel where one person's win becomes the team's knowledge.

## The five sessions

Sessions 1 through 3 build individual AI fluency. The end of Session 3 introduces team-level use. Sessions 4 and 5 move the team from individual to collective fluency.

1. **Kickoff and Mindsets** (2 hrs) — AI-era thinking, tool selection, individual vs. team fluency. (This is Session 0 / the kickoff. The `team-sprint-kickoff` skill covers it, not this one. But Session 1 often carries the same mindset-setting work, as it did for ConForms.)
2. **Getting Great Outputs from AI** (75 min) — prompting and context. The craft of giving AI enough to work with.
3. **AI as Thinking Partner** (75 min) — using AI to analyze, decide, problem-solve. The "big unlock." Team-level use introduced at the end.
4. **Communicating with Impact** (75 min) — tuned to a client-specific thread from discovery (e.g., cross-cultural comms for ConForms, brand voice for Amada).
5. **Redesigning How Work Gets Done** (75 min) — team-level process redesign, done live.

Note: numbering can drift per client. ConForms ran a combined kickoff-plus-content Session 1. Always confirm the actual topic of the session you're working on against the calendar invite and the SOW rather than assuming from the number.

## The six-level adoption ladder (the spine of everything)

Every read on "where the cohort is" and every recap reinforcing progress uses this ladder. Participants see plain-English descriptions, never "Level 3" labels.

| Level | Name | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Not started | Hasn't used AI for anything that mattered, or only failed attempts. |
| 1 | Lookup | AI as a better Google. Quick questions, fact-checking, basic research. |
| 2 | Drafting | AI to produce text. Emails, reports, summaries. Single-shot. Includes voice/dictation. |
| 3 | Thinking partner | AI to analyze, decide, pressure-test. Iterative back-and-forth. The big unlock. |
| 4 | Workflow | AI across multiple steps of one piece of work. Analysis to communication to deliverable. |
| 5 | Automation | Built or uses a reusable tool (custom GPT, agent, skill file) for recurring work. ~6-8 hrs/week saved. |
| 6 | Team redesign | The team changes how work gets done collectively. Shared workflows, peer learning, role redesign. |

**Realistic six-week movement is one to two rungs, not three or four.** Most cohorts start around Level 1-2 and land around Level 3-4. A Level 5 sighting is a real win. Level 6 is Phase 2 territory, and showing that the top of the ladder is still unreached is what makes the next engagement self-evident. Never imply a cohort will "reach the top" in six weeks. That overpromise erodes the trust the whole product runs on.

If you need to code free-response answers to a level, the canonical rubric and anti-inflation rules live in the client's assessment materials (e.g., a `Rubric_Coding_Workflow.md` in their folder). Code to the ceiling a participant actually demonstrates, never to their role or potential, and when torn between two levels, code the lower one.

## The assessment system (light touch for this skill)

The Sprint has a four-touchpoint assessment (pre-Sprint, mid-Sprint pulse, post-Sprint, 60-day) built around the ladder. You don't run the assessment here, but two things matter for session work:

- **Prep mode** may read the latest assessment/rubric data to report where the cohort sits. See `prep-mode.md` for exactly how to source it.
- **Follow-up mode** can reinforce ladder movement in the recap ("most of you are moving from drafting into using AI as a thinking partner"), which doubles as gentle, non-punitive progress visibility. Keep it warm and aggregate, never call out an individual's level.

The deeper design principle to honor: any progress framing is a **progress report, not a sales pitch.** Honesty about where the team still has room to grow is what makes the wins credible.
